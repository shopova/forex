# Impulse Identification Guide

This is a practical checklist for identifying the last clean impulse on the `4H` chart so you can anchor Fibonacci correctly and avoid forcing `1H` triggers inside noise.

The objective is not to label perfect Elliott counts in real time. The objective is to identify the last clean directional leg that shows:

- structure break
- expansion
- follow-through

If that leg is clear, it becomes the swing of interest. If it is not clear, there is no trade.

## 1. Core Definition

A tradable impulse is a directional move that:

- breaks a meaningful prior swing
- moves with visible momentum
- continues in the same direction for several candles
- looks cleaner than the surrounding price action

In this framework:

- `Daily` = macro bias
- `4H` = impulse and correction structure
- `1H` = entry trigger only

You identify the impulse on `4H`, not on `1H`.

## 2. The Fast 5-Point Impulse Test

Use this on every `4H` chart.

If a move passes at least `4 of 5`, it is usually clean enough to use.

### 1. Structure Break

For a bullish impulse:

- price breaks a prior meaningful `4H` swing high

For a bearish impulse:

- price breaks a prior meaningful `4H` swing low

If no meaningful swing is broken, do not call it an impulse.

### 2. Expansion

Look for:

- one or more strong-bodied candles
- widening candle ranges
- clear directional intent

Avoid:

- small mixed candles
- weak drift
- candle-by-candle hesitation

### 3. Follow-Through

After the break, price should keep moving in the same direction for several candles.

Good:

- break, push, small pause, push again

Bad:

- break, immediate stall, full retrace

### 4. Clean Path / Low Overlap

A tradable impulse should be cleaner than the correction around it.

Good:

- limited overlap
- shallow pauses
- correction candles are smaller than push candles

Bad:

- frequent overlap
- alternating candles with no progress
- choppy movement around the same prices

### 5. EMA Alignment

Use the `4H 50 EMA` as the dynamic trend guide.

Bullish:

- price above the `50 EMA`
- `50 EMA` flat-to-rising or clearly rising

Bearish:

- price below the `50 EMA`
- `50 EMA` flat-to-falling or clearly falling

If price is chopping through the `50 EMA`, the market is often not in a clean impulsive state.

## 3. Good vs Bad Impulse

### Good Bullish Impulse

- price is above the `4H 50 EMA`
- a prior `4H` swing high is broken
- one or more expansion candles drive the break
- price continues higher after the break
- pullbacks are brief and smaller than the push
- there is room to the next higher-time-frame resistance

### Good Bearish Impulse

- price is below the `4H 50 EMA`
- a prior `4H` swing low is broken
- one or more expansion candles drive the break
- price continues lower after the break
- bounces are brief and smaller than the push
- there is room to the next higher-time-frame support

### Bad / Non-Tradable Impulse

- no clear swing break
- mostly overlapping candles
- a single spike candle with no continuation
- breakout directly into major nearby structure
- price is stuck around the `4H 50 EMA`
- the move is messy enough that you cannot explain where it started and why it matters

## 4. How To Mark the Impulse

Use this exact routine:

1. Find the last clear `4H` swing low and swing high.
2. Identify which directional move actually broke structure.
3. Mark the start of that move at the last protected swing.
4. Mark the end of that move at the first clear pause before correction begins.
5. Draw Fibonacci only on that completed leg.
6. Do not redraw Fibonacci until a new impulse completes.

This keeps the chart stable and prevents over-analysis.

## 5. How Long an Impulse Leg Usually Is

Typical practical ranges:

- `10-20 candles` = fast, aggressive impulse
- `20-60 candles` = common, clean impulse
- `60-80 candles` = extended but still valid
- `80+ candles` = caution; likely late-stage or overly stretched

On `4H`, your normal lookback for identifying structure is roughly:

- `50-80 candles`

That is enough to define the current impulse and its correction without zooming too far out.

## 6. Impulse vs Correction

The easiest distinction:

Impulse:

- directional
- cleaner
- stronger candles
- less overlap
- breaks structure

Correction:

- slower
- more overlapping
- more back-and-forth
- retraces part of the impulse
- often returns into fib zones or the `50 EMA`

If current price is messy and overlapping, it is usually the correction, not the impulse.

## 7. How This Connects to Your Setups

### Last Kiss

Use only when:

- the impulse already broke out of a clear range or correction
- price accepted outside that structure
- the retest happens after the break, not inside the old chop

Skip when:

- the breakout was only a wick
- price is still inside the old range
- there was no true follow-through after the break

### Wammie / Moolah

Use only when:

- a clear impulse happened first
- price is now correcting into support or resistance
- the correction is slower and more overlapping than the impulse
- the second touch forms at the end of the correction

Skip when:

- the market is still impulsing directly into the zone
- the two touches are just random noise inside chop
- the prior move was not clean enough to define a real correction

### Big Shadow

Use only when:

- a prior impulse stretched price into a real extreme
- the candle forms at a valid zone
- there is room to the left
- it looks like rejection after extension, not random volatility

Skip when:

- it prints in the middle of chop
- there was no prior directional drive into the level
- the candle is large but location is poor

## 8. The 10-Second Decision Rule

Before dropping to `1H`, ask:

1. What was the last clean `4H` impulse?
2. Did it break structure clearly?
3. Is price now correcting, consolidating, or retesting that move?

If you cannot answer those questions quickly and clearly, there is no trade.

## 9. The Weekly Practice Drill

For the next 2 weeks:

1. Open only the `4H` chart.
2. Mark only the last clean impulse on each approved pair.
3. Label it:
   - `clear`
   - `borderline`
   - `noise`
4. Drop to `1H` only on pairs labeled `clear`.
5. Review after the session:
   - Did the correction behave like a correction?
   - Did price respect the fib zone?
   - Was the impulse actually clean, or did you force it?

This will improve setup quality faster than trying to perform full Elliott wave counts in real time.

## 10. Bottom Line

Do not ask:

- "Is this definitely Wave 1, 3, or 5?"

Ask instead:

- Did price break structure?
- Did it expand?
- Did it follow through?
- Is the current phase corrective rather than impulsive?

If yes, you likely have the right anchor leg.

If no, leave the chart alone and wait.
