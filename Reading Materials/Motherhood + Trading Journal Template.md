# Motherhood + Trading Journal Template

This template is designed for a mother of a young child who is managing:

- daily stress
- home load
- emotional regulation
- trading decisions
- body recovery and training

The goal is not perfect journaling.

The goal is:

- reduce pressure
- improve self-awareness
- catch overload early
- make calmer decisions

Use this as a simple daily and weekly template.

## Daily Template

### 1. Morning Grounding (5-7 min)

Write this before checking charts if possible.

```text
Date:

Sleep quality (1-10):
Energy (1-10):
Stress / tightness (1-10):

Baby context today:
(sleep, teething, clingy, calm, disrupted routine, daycare, etc.)

What does my body need today?

What is realistic today?

Today, I do not need to prove anything.
```

Use the "What is realistic today?" line to adjust expectations.

On a hard day, realistic may be:

- no trading
- one analysis only
- a short walk
- stretch only
- basic tasks only

## 2. Midday Check-In (2-4 min)

Use this when you feel pressure building.

```text
What is draining me most right now?

Stress level (1-10):

Where do I feel tension in my body?
(jaw, shoulders, chest, stomach, etc.)

What emotion is strongest right now?

Do I need:
- action
- pause
- food
- water
- movement
- support

Baby note:
(hard nap, clingy, good moment, overstimulated, I lost patience, I handled it well)
```

This is not for judgment.

It is to interrupt stress before it starts driving behavior.

## 3. Pre-Trade / Pre-Decision Reset (3 min)

Use this before any trade or any emotionally loaded decision.

```text
What is the decision?

What is the factual reason?

What is the emotional reason?

If I do nothing, what happens?

Would I still do this if I felt calm?
```

If the honest answer to the last question is no, do not act yet.

## 4. Training / Body Block (2-3 min)

This keeps training supportive, not punishing.

```text
Did I move today?

What kind of movement fits today?
(walk, mobility, strength, stretch, breathing, rest)

Why am I training today?

Supportive reason:
(regulate stress, feel stronger, support energy, feel clear)

Pressure reason:
(punish myself, catch up, force control)
```

If the reason is pressure, reduce the intensity.

## 5. Evening Unload (10 min)

Use this to release pressure before sleep.

```text
What happened today?
(facts only)

What stressed me most?

What did I handle better than before?

Where did I get too tight today?
(with the baby, with myself, while trading, around time, around chores, etc.)

What can I release tonight?
```

Examples for the last prompt:

- I release the need to catch up today.
- I release the guilt about not doing enough.
- I release the pressure to trade on a hard day.
- I release the need to control everything before sleep.

## Weekly Review (20-30 min)

Do this once per week.

### 1. Baby / Home Load

```text
What made the week heavier?

What helped the week feel easier?
```

### 2. Nervous System

```text
When did I feel most reactive?

What helped me regulate?

What made me tighter?
```

### 3. Trading / Work

```text
Did I respect low-capacity days?

Did I force decisions when stressed?

Did I follow my process or react to pressure?
```

### 4. Training / Body

```text
Did movement support me?

Did I rest enough?

Was I using training to care for myself or pressure myself?
```

### 5. Boundaries / Support

```text
What boundary would make next week lighter?

What support do I need?
```

Then choose only one weekly focus.

Examples:

- no phone charts during baby time
- one walk daily
- shorter trading window
- stretch before bed
- no trading on bad-sleep mornings

## Useful Reset Prompts

Use these on hard days:

```text
What am I demanding from myself that does not fit this season?

What would "enough" look like today?

What am I trying to control that is not controllable?

What is urgent, and what only feels urgent?

What would make today feel 10% softer?

What do I need more than productivity right now?
```

## Important Rules

- This journal is not a performance review.
- It is not a place to punish yourself.
- It is not only for bad days.
- It should help you slow down, not tighten up more.

If journaling starts feeling like another task you can fail, shorten it.

Even two honest lines are enough.

## Core Reminder

The purpose of this journal is to help you:

- notice tension
- name it
- lower it
- make calmer decisions

That is success.
