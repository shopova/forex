# Correction Quality Guide

This guide helps you decide whether current price action is a valid correction after a clean `4H` impulse, or just messy structure that should be ignored.

Use it after you have already identified the last clean `4H` impulse.

## 1. Core Idea

A good correction should look weaker than the impulse that came before it.

That usually means:

- slower movement
- smaller candles
- more overlap
- retracement into a logical area
- no major structural invalidation

If the pullback looks as aggressive as the impulse, it is often not a correction anymore.

## 2. Fast 5-Point Correction Test

If a pullback passes at least `4 of 5`, it is usually usable.

### 1. It Retraces, It Does Not Reverse

For a bullish setup:

- price pulls back after a bullish impulse
- the pullback stays above the impulse origin

For a bearish setup:

- price rallies after a bearish impulse
- the pullback stays below the impulse origin

If the prior impulse is structurally invalidated, the correction has failed.

### 2. It Is Slower Than the Impulse

Good correction:

- smaller bodies
- less urgency
- staggered movement

Bad correction:

- large expansion candles against the trend
- sharp momentum that looks like a fresh impulse in the opposite direction

### 3. It Has More Overlap

Good correction:

- more back-and-forth
- overlapping candles
- less clean directional flow

This is one of the easiest visual tells. Corrections usually look messier than impulses.

### 4. It Returns Into a Logical Zone

The best corrections pull back into areas your framework already expects:

- `0.50-0.618` for a deeper wave-3 style continuation setup
- `0.236-0.382` for a shallow wave-5 style continuation setup
- `4H 50 EMA`
- prior breakout / retest area
- obvious `4H` support or resistance

If price is retracing in the middle of nowhere, quality is lower.

### 5. It Preserves Tradeable Space

A valid correction should still leave room to the next higher-time-frame level.

If the pullback returns so deep that:

- the target is crowded
- the next level is too close
- the setup no longer offers at least `2R`

then the correction may be real, but it is not tradeable for your model.

## 3. Good vs Bad Correction

### Good Bullish Correction

After a bullish impulse:

- price pulls back in a controlled way
- candles are smaller than the prior bullish leg
- the pullback overlaps more than the impulse
- price holds above key structure
- price pulls into `0.50-0.618`, `0.236-0.382`, the `4H 50 EMA`, or a prior breakout point

### Good Bearish Correction

After a bearish impulse:

- price rallies in a controlled way
- candles are smaller than the prior bearish leg
- the bounce overlaps more than the impulse
- price holds below key structure
- price rallies into `0.50-0.618`, `0.236-0.382`, the `4H 50 EMA`, or a prior breakdown point

### Bad / Non-Tradeable Correction

- the pullback is as strong as the prior impulse
- price slices through the `4H 50 EMA` with force and keeps going
- price fully erases the prior impulse too quickly
- the correction is happening directly into major opposing structure
- the correction destroys the `2R` profile
- the chart becomes too messy to tell where the correction should end

## 4. Deep vs Shallow Correction

### Deep Correction

Typical zone:

- `0.50-0.618`

Usually means:

- stronger pullback
- better reward if the trend resumes
- more common for wave-3 style continuation

Good signs:

- momentum slows as price enters the zone
- candles compress near the zone
- confluence with EMA or structure exists

### Shallow Correction

Typical zone:

- `0.236-0.382`

Usually means:

- trend is strong
- price does not need to pull back much
- more common for wave-5 style continuation or strong trend continuation

Good signs:

- quick pause near EMA or prior breakout point
- immediate respect of the zone
- no deep penetration into prior structure

## 5. When a Correction Becomes a Reversal Warning

Be careful when:

- the pullback has multiple expansion candles against your trend
- price breaks the structure that should have held
- the `4H 50 EMA` is lost decisively
- the pullback turns into a clean opposite-direction impulse
- macro trend on `Daily` no longer aligns

At that point, stop forcing continuation trades.

## 6. How It Connects to Your Setups

### Last Kiss

Good correction context:

- price broke out first
- pullback returns cleanly to the broken edge
- retest is controlled, not violent

Bad correction context:

- price crashes back into the old box
- multiple closes return inside the broken structure
- no acceptance outside the breakout

### Wammie / Moolah

Good correction context:

- price corrects into a defined zone
- movement into the zone slows down
- second touch forms after the pullback matures

Bad correction context:

- price is still impulsing into the zone
- the move into the zone is too strong and too clean
- the pattern is forming inside broad chop, not after a clear correction

### Big Shadow

Good correction context:

- the prior move is stretched
- the correction reaches a clear extreme
- the rejection candle forms at location, not randomly

Bad correction context:

- price is just oscillating in the middle of range
- there is no clear directional leg behind the setup

## 7. The 10-Second Correction Rule

Before looking for a `1H` trigger, ask:

1. Was there a clean `4H` impulse first?
2. Does the current move look weaker and messier than that impulse?
3. Is price retracing into a logical zone?
4. Is the prior impulse still structurally valid?
5. Is there still at least `2R` to the next meaningful level?

If you cannot answer yes to most of these, there is no setup.

## 8. Practical Drill

For each approved pair:

1. Mark the last clean `4H` impulse.
2. Watch the next phase only.
3. Label the next phase:
   - `healthy correction`
   - `too deep`
   - `too aggressive`
   - `random chop`
4. Only drop to `1H` when it is clearly a healthy correction.

This will improve your entry selection faster than focusing on candle triggers alone.

## 9. Bottom Line

A good correction should make you think:

- "the trend paused"

Not:

- "the market may have fully flipped"

If the pullback feels too strong, too clean, or too destructive, treat it as a warning, not as an entry opportunity.
