# Forex Living Guide for an EUR80,000 Account

This is an operating guide for trading professionally with an EUR80,000 account using the structure-first framework already documented in this repository. It is designed for roughly 2-3 hours of focused work per day across analysis, news, execution, review, and education.

It is not a promise of profits. No trading plan can guarantee a positive balance every month. The objective is to maximize the probability of long-term profitability while protecting capital and keeping the process realistic.

## 1. Start With the Right Math

If you want to average EUR4,000 per month from an EUR80,000 account, you are targeting:

- EUR4,000 / EUR80,000 = 5% per month
- 60% per year before compounding, slippage, spreads, and taxes

That is possible in strong periods, but aggressive as a long-run average for a discretionary retail trader. A more realistic professional target is:

- Baseline: +1.5% to +3.0% average monthly over a rolling 6-12 month period
- Strong months: +4% to +6%
- Flat or negative months: expected and normal

What this means in euros:

- 1.5% = EUR1,200
- 2.0% = EUR1,600
- 3.0% = EUR2,400
- 5.0% = EUR4,000

If EUR4,000 per month is the amount you need to live on, the safer path is either:

- grow capital first, or
- combine trading income with another income source until your track record is stable

More durable capital targets for EUR4,000/month:

- EUR130,000 account at 3.0% average = EUR3,900
- EUR160,000 account at 2.5% average = EUR4,000
- EUR200,000 account at 2.0% average = EUR4,000

## 2. Use the Repository's Risk Logic, But Keep the Stricter Version

Your documents contain both aggressive and conservative risk references. For an EUR80,000 account intended to become income-producing, use the stricter controls as the default.

Core risk limits:

- Risk per trade: 0.25% to 0.50%
- Daily max loss: 1.00%
- Max concurrent open risk: 0.75%
- Minimum planned reward: 2R
- No new entries within 1 hour before or after high-impact news

Translated into euros:

- 0.25% risk = EUR200
- 0.50% risk = EUR400
- 0.75% total open risk cap = EUR600
- 1.00% daily stop = EUR800

Practical rule set:

- Standard setup risk: 0.25%
- A-grade setup risk: 0.50%
- Never increase risk because you are behind on the month
- If you hit daily max loss, stop trading for the day
- If you hit 3 consecutive losses or 4% drawdown from equity high, cut risk in half until you recover process quality

## 3. Understand What the EUR4,000 Goal Requires

At 0.50% risk, 1R equals EUR400.

Examples:

- One full 2R winner = EUR800
- One 1R loser = -EUR400

To make EUR4,000 in a month at 0.50% risk, you need about +10R net.

That is achievable, but only if you maintain solid expectancy and enough quality trade flow.

Example expectancy:

- Win rate: 45%
- Average win: 2R
- Average loss: 1R
- Expectancy: (0.45 x 2R) - (0.55 x 1R) = +0.35R per trade

At 0.50% risk, +0.35R is about EUR140 expected value per trade.

At that expectancy, EUR4,000 requires about 29 trades in a month. That is a meaningful workload for a selective, rules-based trader. It is one reason you should treat EUR4,000 as a stretch average, not a forced monthly quota.

The correct operating goal is:

- Protect capital first
- Build a statistically positive process
- Withdraw only from genuine edge, not from pressure

## 4. Trading Style That Fits 2-3 Hours Per Day

Your documented workflow is already compatible with limited screen time. Keep it that way.

Best fit:

- Trade one session only: London or New York, never both
- Default to swing-style execution: Daily bias, 4H structure, 1H trigger
- Use alerts and pending stop orders instead of watching charts all day
- Hold trades for multiple hours to multiple days when the structure supports it

This is not a scalp-heavy model. The edge comes from waiting for high-quality structure, not from constant screen time.

## 5. Daily Operating Schedule (2-3 Hours Total)

### Option A: Split Schedule

This is the cleanest model for a professional routine.

Block 1: Pre-session and execution (75-105 minutes)

- 15-20 min: mindset check, review open trades, review yesterday's mistakes
- 10-15 min: check economic calendar and central bank headlines
- 20-30 min: scan approved pairs on Daily and 4H
- 20-30 min: mark zones, fibs, invalidation, and set alerts or stop orders
- 10 min: document your plan before the session starts

Block 2: Review and education (30-60 minutes later in the day)

- 10-15 min: review whether price respected your levels
- 10-15 min: journal executed or missed setups
- 15-30 min: education block (one specific topic only)

### Option B: Single Concentrated Block

If your schedule only allows one block:

- 20 min: news and calendar
- 40 min: multi-timeframe analysis
- 20 min: execution planning and order placement
- 20 min: journaling and screenshot capture
- 20-30 min: education/review

## 6. What to Do During Those Hours

### A. News and Macro Awareness (15-20 minutes)

You do not need to become a macro economist. You do need to know when not to trade and when volatility is likely to expand.

Track only what materially affects your pairs:

- Central bank decisions and speeches: ECB, Federal Reserve, Bank of England, Bank of Japan
- Inflation: CPI
- Labor: NFP, unemployment
- Growth: GDP
- Business activity: PMI

What to do:

- Mark the day's high-impact events before your scan
- Avoid fresh entries 60 minutes before and after top-tier releases
- If already in a trade, decide in advance whether you will hold, reduce, or move to breakeven

Use official sources first:

- Federal Reserve FOMC calendar
- ECB monetary policy calendar
- Bank of England MPC and news pages
- BLS release calendar for CPI and payrolls

### B. Pair Selection (10 minutes)

Stay narrow. Your own docs already point to this.

Primary pairs:

- EURUSD
- GBPUSD
- Optional: USDJPY
- Optional: USDCAD or AUDUSD

Why this is the correct universe:

- They are among the most liquid and most followed FX markets
- The BIS 2025 Triennial Survey reported that USD was on one side of 89.2% of global FX turnover, which reinforces focusing on USD majors
- They align with your current strategy documentation and backtesting scope
- A smaller universe improves pattern recognition and reduces random trades

Do not build an "always in a trade" lifestyle by adding too many pairs.

### C. Structured Chart Work (30-45 minutes)

Use the same sequence every day:

1. Daily chart:
- Is price clearly above or below the 200 SMA?
- Is the market trending or overlapping?
- Where are the obvious major levels?

2. 4H chart:
- Identify the last clean impulse
- Draw the fib on that impulse only
- Prefer:
  - 0.50-0.618 retracement for wave-3 style continuation
  - 0.236-0.382 retracement for wave-5 style continuation
- Confirm alignment with the 50 EMA and clean structure

3. 1H chart:
- Wait only for a valid trigger
- Use price action, not prediction

### D. Execution (10-20 minutes)

Only execute when all layers agree:

- Trend filter
- Structure
- Fib location
- News clearance
- Clean trigger
- Minimum 2R available before the next major barrier

Use:

- Buy stop / sell stop entries
- Stop loss at structural invalidation plus an ATR buffer if needed
- Automatic position sizing based on the stop distance

Cancel untriggered orders after 3-5 candles unless the structure is still clearly valid.

### E. Review and Education (20-45 minutes)

This is part of trading, not extra.

Use the education block for only one purpose each day:

- Review one losing trade in detail
- Replay one textbook setup
- Re-read one section of your handbook
- Compare planned vs actual execution
- Update your journal statistics

Avoid "content consumption" that feels productive but does not improve your edge. Random videos, social media market takes, and constant Telegram or Discord chatter are usually noise.

## 7. Keep the Entry Model Simple

You already have a defined setup family. Do not dilute it.

Your primary 1H triggers:

- Last Kiss
- Wammie / Moolah
- Big Shadow

Use them only when they appear in the right location:

- Trend must already be established on higher timeframes
- The setup must occur at a valid structure or fib zone
- The invalidation must be obvious
- The reward must be at least 2R

Practical priority:

- Last Kiss: best for breakout-retest continuation in a strong trend
- Wammie / Moolah: best for reversal at high-quality support or resistance after correction
- Big Shadow: best used selectively, only at strong extremes and only with clean room to the left

If you want a confirmation layer in stronger trends, QQE/QMP can be used as a secondary timing filter, but it should not override the higher-timeframe structure and risk rules.

## 8. Monthly Performance Framework

Judge the month by process and R, not by emotion.

Suggested monthly scorecard:

- Number of trades
- Win rate
- Average R winner
- Average R loser
- Net R
- Rule violations
- Missed A-grade setups
- Largest drawdown

Healthy process targets:

- Rule violations: 0-2 maximum
- Average reward on winners: 1.8R or better
- Net month: positive over a rolling 3-month sample, not necessarily every single month

Income-focused interpretation:

- Below +2R: capital preserved, review required
- +3R to +6R: solid professional month
- +7R to +10R: strong month
- 10R+: excellent month, but do not assume it is repeatable every month

At 0.50% risk:

- +3R = EUR1,200
- +6R = EUR2,400
- +10R = EUR4,000

## 9. Withdrawal Rules If You Want to Live From Trading

The fastest way to cripple a good account is to force monthly withdrawals too early.

Recommended withdrawal policy:

- First 6 months: no fixed income withdrawals
- Build a rolling 6-month track record first
- Withdraw only a fraction of profits, not a fixed salary from equity

Practical model:

- If equity is below the high-water mark: withdraw nothing
- If monthly performance is below +3R: withdraw nothing
- If monthly performance is +3R to +6R: withdraw up to 25% of profits
- If monthly performance is above +6R: withdraw up to 33% of profits
- Leave the rest in the account to absorb drawdowns and compound

This protects both psychology and account durability.

## 10. What "Making a Living" Should Mean in Practice

A professional trader does not define success as "I need EUR4,000 this month no matter what."

A professional trader defines success as:

- I followed my process
- I protected capital
- I avoided low-quality trades
- I produced positive expectancy over a large sample
- I can keep doing this next month

The sustainable path to living from Forex is usually:

1. Build 6-12 months of live consistency
2. Keep risk small and stable
3. Scale only after evidence, not after hope
4. Increase capital before increasing lifestyle dependence

## 11. A Realistic 90-Day Plan

### Month 1: Stabilize Process

- Use 0.25% risk on standard setups
- Use 0.50% risk only on your cleanest A-grade setups
- Focus only on EURUSD and GBPUSD
- Track every trade in R
- No income withdrawals

Goal:

- zero revenge trading
- zero overtrading
- zero risk-rule breaches

### Month 2: Tighten Execution

- Keep the same pair list
- Remove your weakest setup type if its stats lag
- Reduce hesitation by pre-defining alerts and stop orders
- Spend more education time on screenshots of your own trades, not outside content

Goal:

- improve execution quality, not frequency

### Month 3: Evaluate for Scale

- Review 30-60 trades if available
- Keep only setups with positive expectancy
- Decide whether your current edge supports 0.50% as normal risk or only for top-tier setups

Goal:

- know your real numbers before you treat this as income

## 12. Hard Rules That Protect the Account

- No trade if Daily and 4H are neutral or messy
- No entry if the setup is inside chop
- No entry if there is no clear invalidation
- No entry if reward is below 2R
- No entry because you "need" a win
- No widening stops
- No adding to losers
- No doubling risk after a losing streak
- No second session because the first session was quiet

## 13. Bottom Line

With an EUR80,000 account, a disciplined, structure-based Forex process can reasonably aim to become a serious income stream. What is realistic, however, is not the same as what is possible in a best-case month.

If you force EUR4,000 every month, you will likely overtrade or over-risk. If you target process quality, protect capital, and let returns vary, the account has a real chance to grow into something you can live from.

The correct objective is:

- first, become consistently profitable
- second, become consistently repeatable
- third, convert profits into income only after the track record is proven
