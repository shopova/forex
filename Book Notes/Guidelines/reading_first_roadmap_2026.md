# Reading-First Roadmap 2026

## Objective

This roadmap is for the phase before active FTMO preparation.

Its purpose is not to consume many books.

Its purpose is to improve:

- market-condition reading
- level quality judgment
- pullback quality judgment
- valid opportunity identification
- execution realism
- risk and expectancy thinking

## Decision Rules For Including A Book

A book goes into the core curriculum only if it helps at least one of these:

1. classify market condition better
2. identify valid location and invalidation better
3. understand why a setup is strong or weak
4. improve trade management and expectancy thinking
5. improve execution discipline and journaling

A book gets downgraded if:

- it is mostly setup marketing
- it introduces many patterns but weak filtering logic
- it relies on charisma more than framework
- it gives ideas without helping you reject bad trades
- it overlaps heavily with a stronger book you already have

## Core Reading Principle

Your edge will improve more from these skills than from collecting new triggers:

- reading `trend / range / transition`
- spotting healthy vs failed pullbacks
- seeing whether a level is meaningful or random
- separating continuation from exhaustion
- defining invalidation before entry
- filtering out mid-range noise

## Core Curriculum

### Tier 1: Must Read Carefully

These are the books that directly improve your edge.

1. `The Art and Science of Technical Analysis`
   - best book in your library for market structure, pullbacks, transitions, failed reversals, and practical templates
   - this should be the backbone of your chart reading
2. `The New Trading for a Living`
   - best bridge between psychology, practical trading process, trade management, and record keeping
   - especially useful for building a professional operating routine
3. `Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom`
   - essential for expectancy, system design, and position sizing
   - this improves trade selection indirectly by forcing you to think in edge and `R`
4. `Forex Patterns and Probabilities`
   - useful because it is FX-specific and focused on trend vs range context
   - good companion to the structure work, not a replacement for it
5. `Technical Analysis of the Financial Markets`
   - use as a reference text, not cover-to-cover reading
   - strongest use is building broad chart literacy and classic structural vocabulary

### Tier 2: Read Selectively

These are useful, but only after Tier 1.

1. `Trading Price Action Trends`
   - useful for trend leg quality and continuation behavior
   - read selectively because it is dense and can become too micro if taken too literally
2. `High Probability Trading Strategies`
   - useful for structure-to-execution thinking
   - read for filtering and planning, not for copying setups
3. `The Disciplined Trader`
   - useful if hesitation, fear, or self-sabotage remain strong after reading Douglas and Elder
4. `Day Trading and Swing Trading the Currency Market`
   - useful as an FX-specific contextual book
   - lower priority than the books above, but still worthwhile

### Tier 3: Read For Vocabulary, Not For Foundation

These books can still help, but they should not define your framework.

1. `Naked Forex`
   - useful for setup vocabulary and chart-based thinking
   - not strong enough to be your primary framework
   - treat it as a source of examples, not doctrine
2. `Dave Landry on Swing Trading`
   - useful but narrower
3. `Japanese Candlestick Charting Techniques`
   - useful for candle language, but candles are secondary to context

## Books To Deprioritize

Do not spend prime study energy on these now:

- `17 Proven Currency Trading Strategies`
- `Forex Trading Secrets`
- `How to Make a Living Trading Foreign Exchange`
- books that promise many strategies quickly
- books where the main value is storytelling rather than framework

These may contain some useful ideas, but they are low-leverage for your current problem.

## What To Learn From Each Core Book

### 1. The Art and Science of Technical Analysis

Extract:

- trend structure
- healthy pullback vs failed pullback
- trend vs range vs transition
- breakout vs failed breakout
- support and resistance as functional structures
- practical continuation and failure-test templates

Do not read it passively.

Every session should answer:

- what is the market doing
- what would make this setup valid
- what would make it invalid

### 2. The New Trading for a Living

Extract:

- multi-timeframe organization
- trader self-management
- risk planning
- rating your trades
- journaling and review process

This book is valuable because it helps build an operating system, not because it gives magical entries.

### 3. Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom

Extract:

- expectancy
- position sizing
- system logic
- how an edge is evaluated
- why good entries alone are not enough

Read this as a design manual.

### 4. Forex Patterns and Probabilities

Extract:

- FX-specific behavior
- trend/range distinctions
- how to think in probabilities rather than pattern certainty
- practical chart scenarios around continuation and range conditions

### 5. Technical Analysis of the Financial Markets

Extract selectively:

- trends
- support/resistance
- reversal and continuation patterns
- moving averages
- money management and trading tactics
- intermarket logic only as background

Use this like a reference dictionary for chart language.

## Honest View On Naked Forex

Your instinct is reasonable.

`Naked Forex` is not useless, but it is overrated if used as a full trading education.

Its best value is:

- simple setup language
- chart-based thinking
- reminder to focus on price rather than indicator clutter

Its limitations are:

- not enough depth on market condition
- not enough depth on when to reject a pattern
- not enough depth on building a durable professional process

So the right place for it is:

- secondary source
- setup vocabulary
- example library

Not:

- your main framework book

## Reading-Heavy First Period

For your roadmap, the first period should be reading-heavy, but not reading-only.

Use this ratio:

- `60%` reading and note-making
- `25%` chart review
- `15%` light historical replay / backtest observation

If you do reading without charts, very little will stick.

## Weekly Study Structure

### Week A

- `4` reading sessions
- `2` chart annotation sessions
- `1` summary and concept extraction session

### Week B

- same structure, but with one extra replay / screenshot review block

Repeat.

## Required Output Per Book

You are not allowed to finish a book without producing:

1. `one-page concept summary`
2. `one-page chart checklist`
3. `three examples of valid opportunities`
4. `three examples of invalid opportunities`
5. `one rule that changes your process`

If a book cannot produce those outputs, it is probably not helping your edge enough.
