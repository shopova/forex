Parent chapter:
- `Chapter 3: On Trends`

Section focus:
- `The Fundamental Pattern`

## Core Idea

- Trends are built from alternating impulse and pullback.
- The trader's job is to read whether that rhythm is healthy, weakening, or broken.
- A chart becomes dangerous when I stop seeing the impulse-pullback structure and start forcing trend labels anyway.

## Key Points

- A clean trend is not a straight line.
- Impulse should show conviction and directional imbalance.
- Pullback should correct, not destroy, the prior impulse.
- The market's structure matters more than any single candle.

## What To Look For

- `Clean signs`
  - strong directional push
  - orderly pullback
  - continuation attempt that has room
- `Warning signs`
  - overlapping bars
  - weak impulse
  - pullback that becomes too aggressive
- `Disqualifying signs`
  - no readable swing rhythm
  - supposed trend is really a range or transition

## Trading Meaning

- Best use:
  - `Continue with trend only when the impulse-pullback rhythm is still intact.`
- Avoid:
  - `Treating every pullback as opportunity when the structure is already degrading.`

## Rule Extracted

- `If I cannot clearly see impulse and pullback, I should stop calling the chart a clean trend.`

## Practical Forex Translation

- Your continuation setups should come after real impulse, not after drift.
- If the chart does not have clear rhythm, it is lower quality for `Last Kiss` and other continuation logic.
