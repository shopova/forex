## Main Takeaway

- Chapter 9 is about separating normal trading costs from true account-threatening risk.
- Good risk management is not only about using stops. It is about sizing correctly, thinking in `R`, respecting drawdown math, and avoiding hidden risks that can destroy the account even when the chart idea looked valid.
- The main job is survival first:
  - define loss before entry
  - size from that loss
  - avoid oversized exposure, correlation, and edge decay

## Section Coverage

- `Risk and Position Sizing`
  - Stops and position size must be linked from the start.
  - Fixed-fractional risk is practical because it controls downside and scales with equity.
  - Higher per-trade risk can increase returns, but drawdowns and ruin risk rise faster than most traders expect.

- `Misunderstood Risk`
  - Traders often confuse normal business expense with true risk.
  - Over many trades, the real objective is positive expected value, not emotional comfort on one trade.
  - Probability, consequence, and asymmetry matter more than simplistic ideas like "low risk" or "high reward/risk."

- `Practical Risks in Trading`
  - Some of the biggest real risks are not on the chart:
    - no real edge
    - execution friction
    - disaster / infrastructure failure
    - market evolution
    - tail risk
    - correlation
    - liquidity
    - regulatory change

## What This Chapter Changes

- I should stop treating risk as only "where is my stop?"
- Every trade decision has to answer three questions:
  - where am I wrong?
  - how much of the account is at risk?
  - what other hidden risks make this trade bigger than it appears?
- Small, repeatable risk with controlled downside matters more than chasing larger returns through bigger size.

## Practical Forex Translation

- In FX, correlation risk is easy to underestimate:
  - multiple USD trades can behave like one oversized bet
  - session-wide exposure can become concentrated very quickly
- The stop must come from structure first, then lot size must be adjusted to fit account risk.
- News, spread expansion, thin liquidity, and execution hesitation are real trading frictions that can turn a technically valid setup into a bad live trade.
- This chapter strongly supports your current rule stack:
  - fixed risk
  - structural invalidation
  - minimum acceptable `R`
  - skipping trades when conditions do not justify clean execution

## Rule Extracted

- `Risk management is not about avoiding losses; it is about making sure no normal loss, rare shock, or sizing mistake can put me out of business.`
