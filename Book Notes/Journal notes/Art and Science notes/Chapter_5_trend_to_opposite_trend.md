Section focus:
- `Trend to Opposite Trend (Trend Reversal)`

## Core Idea

- A direct reversal from one trend into the opposite trend is less common than a trend ending into range.
- When it happens, the market usually shows a strong `change of character`, not just a minor loss of momentum.
- The chapter highlights two especially important reversal contexts: `parabolic climax` and `last gasp`.

## Parabolic Blow-Off / Climax

- Trend accelerates beyond normal behavior.
- Multiple steepening legs or one dramatic inflection into an unsustainable move.
- Everyone who wants in gets in near the top or bottom.
- Once that pressure stops, the market collapses into the vacuum and sharp countertrend momentum emerges.

## Expectations After Climax

- Common sequence:
  - sharp countertrend move
  - consolidation
  - another strong leg in the countertrend direction
- Sometimes the result is only extended consolidation, not instant sustained reversal.
- If two sharp impulse moves hit in opposite directions back-to-back, triangle-like consolidation is likely and often not worth trading.

## Last Gasp

- After enough consolidation near an old extreme, the market appears ready for one more leg.
- Price pokes through the old extreme, triggers breakout buying or stop-outs, then immediately fails.
- This is essentially a special-case `upthrust` or `spring`, but with more power because it follows a structure that looked ready to continue.

## Change of Character

- This is the unifying concept.
- The old trend behavior breaks.
- Price starts moving differently: larger countertrend swings, sharper momentum the other way, new imbalance.
- Sometimes it is as simple as: `Hey, that's different.`

## Trend Change Without Warning

- Rare, but important.
- Usually driven by new information or a major catalyst.
- Strong trends normally do not stop and reverse instantly, so when they do, pay attention.

## What This Changes For Me

- Do not call reversal only because trend looks overextended.
- Require `change of character`, not just emotion.
- Respect lower-time-frame climax when it aligns with higher-time-frame structure.

## Misread Risk

- Easy mistake:
  - Calling every exhaustion move a major reversal when many only become consolidation first.
- What still needs practice:
  - Distinguishing a tradable climax reversal from a dramatic move that still needs more time to resolve.

## Rule Extracted

- `A true trend reversal needs change of character. Exhaustion alone is not enough.`

## Practical Forex Translation

- `Big Shadow` near a clear climax or `last gasp` can matter a lot more than the same candle in the middle of normal structure.
- A lower-time-frame exhaustion into a higher-time-frame level can create an excellent entry, but only when both time frames align.
- For your strategy, this is where aggressive reversals belong: rare, selective, and only with very strong evidence.
