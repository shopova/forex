- `Active Management`

## Core Idea

- Active management is one of the main reasons to trade discretionary methods, but it can also become a source of random damage.
- The key question is not whether I can interact with the trade, but whether my interaction actually adds value.

## Main Trade Management Decisions

- `Enter all at once or scale in`
  - Scaling in can make sense in some contexts, but risk must be capped from the start.

- `Take partial profits`
  - Improves consistency but reduces size on the biggest winners.
  - Good only if the rule is planned and repeatable.

- `Take partial losses`
  - Often dangerous because it can anchor the P&L on the loss side.
  - Usually better to exit the whole trade than to randomly deleverage a losing position.

- `Add to existing trades`
  - Can work, but only with a clear plan.
  - Reverse pyramids are especially dangerous and can destroy accounts.
  - If adding, smaller and more controlled additions are superior to emotional size-ups.

- `Time stops`
  - Very useful when the setup should work quickly.
  - If the trade stays flat too long, the edge may already be gone.

## Grimes' Bias

- Good discretionary traders can outperform rigid trailing-stop rules, but only if they are consistent and actually reading structure well.
- Tools like Parabolic SAR or chandelier stops can help as references, but should not replace understanding.
- Many traders add randomness, not value, when they manage trades actively under pressure.

## What This Changes For Me

- Every management action should be tied to a reason:
  - reducing risk
  - paying myself
  - responding to changing structure
  - removing a dead trade
- If I cannot explain the reason clearly before the trade, I probably should not be doing it during the trade.

## Rule Extracted

- `Active management should remove uncertainty from the trade, not add new random decisions to it.`

## Practical Forex Translation

- This chapter strongly supports your session-based, structured approach.
- Time stops and partial-profit rules are especially relevant for you because you do not want positions lingering without behaving correctly.
- The biggest practical takeaway for your framework:
  - plan partials, stop movement, and trade cancellation before entry, not during stress.
