## Main Takeaway

- Chapter 6 turns the structure work from Chapters 2-5 into executable trade templates.
- The core building blocks are still simple: `pullback`, `failure test`, and `breakout`.
- The other patterns are combinations or context-specific versions of those same building blocks.
- The practical edge comes from knowing where the trade belongs in structure, how it should behave if correct, and what early failure should look like.

## Section Coverage

- `Failure Test`
  - Fast countertrend rejection at a key level with very clear risk.

- `Pullback, Buying Support or Shorting Resistance`
  - Classic continuation trade entered near the edge of the pullback itself.

- `Pullback, Entering Lower Time Frame Breakout`
  - Continuation entry that sacrifices price location for momentum confirmation.

- `Trading Complex Pullbacks`
  - Second-leg pullback structures that often stop out simple pullback traders but can offer strong continuation entries.

- `The Anti`
  - First pullback after a potential trend change; a structured way to trade possible reversal without pure guessing.

- `Breakouts, Entering in the Preceding Base`
  - Anticipatory breakout entry taken before the actual break to improve location and avoid breakout slippage.

- `Breakouts, Entering on First Pullback Following`
  - Post-breakout continuation entry that treats the first reaction as a standard pullback.

- `Failed Breakouts`
  - Breakout failure traded as a failed pullback / support-resistance holding event.

## Primary Trades vs Derived Trades

- `Primary trades`
  - `Pullback`
  - `Failure test`
  - `Breakout`

- `Derived trades`
  - `Anti`
  - buying support in pullback
  - lower-time-frame breakout inside pullback
  - breakout entry in base
  - breakout entry on first pullback
  - failed breakout

## What This Chapter Changes

- I should stop thinking in terms of many unrelated setups and instead see a small set of repeatable structural patterns.
- Every setup needs a behavioral expectation:
  - what should happen quickly if I am right
  - what should not happen if I am right
- Failure patterns matter as much as winning patterns because they define exits, reentries, and setup quality.

## Practical Forex Translation

- `Last Kiss` sits closest to breakout continuation and first-pullback-following-breakout logic.
- `Big Shadow` can overlap with `failure test`, `Anti`, or exhaustion/reversal context.
- `Wammie/Moolah` often overlaps with `failure test` and trapped-trader logic.
- Your continuation framework maps most directly to the `pullback` family; your reversal framework should be much more selective and closer to `failure test` or `Anti`.

## Rule Extracted

- `Trade templates are just structure plus behavior. If I cannot define both, I do not really have a setup.`
