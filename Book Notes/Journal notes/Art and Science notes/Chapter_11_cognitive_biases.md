# Section Note: Art and Science of Technical Analysis

Source:
- `Reading-materials/some-investment-books/New Folder/The Art and Science of Technical Analysis - Market Structure, Price Action, and Trading Strategies 2012 - annotated.pdf`

Parent chapter:
- `Chapter 11: The Trader's Mind`

Section focus:
- `Cognitive Biases`

## Core Idea

- Traders do not approach markets as neutral observers.
- Built-in biases distort how they interpret streaks, losses, probabilities, outcomes, and their own skill.

## Main Lessons

- `Gambler's fallacy`
  - A string of losses does not mean a win is due.
  - Markets can also have regime effects, so this must be understood carefully rather than emotionally.

- `Loss asymmetry`
  - Traders often lock in small gains too quickly and hold losers too long.
  - Emotional comfort and expected value are not the same thing.

- `Overconfidence`
  - Traders routinely overestimate their skill and underestimate randomness.
  - This is especially dangerous after short runs of success.

- `Hindsight bias`
  - After the trade, everything looks clearer and more obvious than it was in real time.
  - This can corrupt review quality.

- `Illusion of control`
  - Traders often mistake activity and choice for actual influence over outcomes.
  - If there is no real edge, feeling in control changes nothing.

## What This Changes For Me

- I should distrust conclusions drawn from recent streaks.
- I should review trades based on the information available at entry and management time, not with hindsight.
- Confidence needs to come from tested process, not from feeling sharp.

## Rule Extracted

- `Biases make bad decisions feel rational, so I need written rules and sample-based review to challenge my own conclusions.`

## Practical Forex Translation

- This applies directly to:
  - increasing size after a few losers because a win feels overdue
  - moving stops because I "know" the trade will come back
  - believing a recent good week proves the method is solved
  - reviewing a missed move and acting as if the setup was obvious live
- The fix is not better confidence. It is cleaner data, better journaling, and stricter adherence to pre-trade criteria.
