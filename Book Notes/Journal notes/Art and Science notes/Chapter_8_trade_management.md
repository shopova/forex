## Main Takeaway

- Chapter 8 is about turning a valid setup into a controlled trade plan.
- Stops, targets, and management decisions are not interchangeable; they must match the actual behavior and time horizon of the pattern being traded.
- Random discretion after entry is dangerous. Structure, volatility, and expectancy should drive management choices.

## Section Coverage

- `Placing the Initial Stop`
  - Stop placement defines risk and position size from the start.
  - Fixed-percentage stops are crude; structure- and volatility-aware stops are better.

- `Setting Price Targets`
  - Targets can be based on `R` multiples, market structure, or dynamic trailing logic.
  - The target must respect the real predictive horizon of the setup.

- `Active Management`
  - Active management can add value, but only if it is rule-based and consistent.
  - Partial exits, adds, pyramids, and time stops all involve trade-offs that should be planned in advance.

## What This Chapter Changes

- I should stop thinking of trade management as something improvised after entry.
- The management plan must match the setup:
  - short-lived edge needs tighter target logic
  - longer-lived edge needs room
- Every trade should have:
  - clear initial stop
  - realistic first target
  - rule for what happens if the trade stalls

## Practical Forex Translation

- Your stop placement should come from structure and volatility, not a fixed pip count.
- Your `2R` preference fits well with Grimes' discussion of taking partial profits while still leaving some room for larger moves.
- Time stops are especially relevant for your session-based execution model: if the trade cannot behave as expected soon enough, the edge may be gone.

## Rule Extracted

- `A good setup can still lose money if the stop, target, and management logic do not match the actual behavior of that setup.`
