# Section Note: Art and Science of Technical Analysis

Parent chapter:
- `Chapter 6: Practical Trading Templates`

Section focus:
- `Failed Breakouts`

## Core Idea

- Most breakouts fail.
- The failure of the first pullback after breakout is often the real trigger for the failed-breakout trade.
- This trade is best understood as a failed pullback / support-resistance holding event.

## Setup

- Two key conditions:
  - weak character of the move beyond the breakout level
  - weak / disproportional first reaction after breakout
- Good breakout should show conviction and a controlled pullback.
- If the first reaction is too strong against the breakout, failure probability rises sharply.

## Trigger

- The actual trade trigger is confirmation that the first pullback has failed.
- The challenge is balancing:
  - enough confirmation
  - still acceptable trade location

## Stop

- Ultimate stop is beyond the extreme of the initial pullback thrust.
- This is a high-volatility, high-tail-risk area.
- If the stop is hit, get out. No averaging, no games.

## Profit Target

- Two common outcomes:
  - failure absorbed into a larger consolidation
  - sharp collapse / squeeze as trapped traders unwind
- Sensible plan:
  - take early partial profits
  - keep a remainder for a larger move if panic accelerates

## Main Challenge

- This is one of the most subjective and dangerous templates in the chapter.
- It requires strong understanding of both breakout and pullback behavior.
- Newer traders are usually better off studying it first and trading it later.

## Rule Extracted

- `A failed breakout is usually revealed by the failure of the first pullback after the breakout, not by the first simple retest of the breakout level.`

## Practical Forex Translation

- This maps well to false-break box logic and failed continuation attempts around obvious levels.
- For your framework, it is valuable both as a trade idea and as protection against staying in weak breakout trades too long.
