Section focus:
- `Setting Price Targets`

## Core Idea

- There is no single right answer for targets.
- The real question is whether the target respects:
  - the predictive power of the pattern
  - the structure of the market
  - the trader's personality

## Main Approaches

- `Risk-multiple targets`
  - Grimes uses partial exits around `1R` and then manages the remainder.
  - This is disciplined and easy to integrate into a broader plan.

- `Market-structure targets`
  - Visible pivots, support/resistance, session highs/lows, or higher-time-frame levels.
  - Targets usually make more sense slightly inside obvious levels, not beyond them.

- `Trailing / open-ended targets`
  - Best when a real trend may keep extending.
  - Should still be governed by a plan, not by hope.

## Key Principle

- The profit target must match the setup's actual edge horizon.
- A pattern with predictive value for only a few bars should not be managed like a multiweek trend-following system.
- There is usually only a small `sweet spot` where stop/target choices fit the pattern well.

## What This Changes For Me

- I should not force big targets onto short-lived edges.
- Structure matters:
  - if my target sits beyond an obvious pivot, it may be unrealistic
- Partial profit logic can balance consistency with the chance of larger winners.

## Rule Extracted

- `My target must respect the actual reach of the pattern, not just what I want the trade to pay.`

## Practical Forex Translation

- This fits your `minimum 2R` filter well, but with one warning:
  - if `2R` sits beyond obvious structure with no room, the trade may be invalid even if the candle looks good
- A practical plan is:
  - partial near `1R`
  - manage the rest only if structure and room justify it
