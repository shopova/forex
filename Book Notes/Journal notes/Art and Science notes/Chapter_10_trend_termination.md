Section focus:
- `Trend Termination`

## Core Idea

- Trend termination trades are controlled attempts to catch the end of an exhausted move.
- They are harder than continuation trades because volatility is higher, failure risk is larger, and reversals often do not travel as far as traders imagine.
- The key pattern is the failure test after a real change of character.

## Main Lessons

- `Do not fade trends blindly`
  - Countertrend trades need justification.
  - The best reason is a visible break in the prior trend behavior:
    - failed continuation
    - change of character
    - overextension
    - climactic conditions

- `Failure tests offer disciplined entries`
  - The market probes to a new extreme, then rejects it.
  - Entry can be on the same bar or the following bar, but discipline matters more than perfect timing.

- `These trades deserve smaller risk`
  - Stops are often tight.
  - Gap risk and violent continuation against the position are real.
  - Smaller size is often more sensible than standard continuation size.

- `Take the first target seriously`
  - Partial profits at `1R` are essential.
  - Many reversal attempts briefly work, hit the first target, then resume the old trend.

## What This Changes For Me

- A countertrend setup is not valid just because a market looks extended.
- I need evidence that the old pressure is weakening before I even think about a reversal trade.
- If I do take one, it should be managed more defensively than a with-trend setup.

## Rule Extracted

- `Only trade trend termination after a real change of character, and pay myself early because reversals are fragile.`

## Practical Forex Translation

- In FX, this means:
  - do not short simply because price is high or buy simply because price is low
  - wait for failure at the extreme, not just extension into it
  - treat reversal attempts around session extremes or major daily levels with smaller size and faster risk reduction
- This section supports your preference for structure-led entries over emotional fading.
