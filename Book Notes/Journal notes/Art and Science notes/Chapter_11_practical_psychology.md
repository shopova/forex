Section focus:
- `Practical Psychology`

## Core Idea

- Good psychology in trading is mostly practical design:
  - choose a style that fits
  - reduce unnecessary stress
  - build consistent routines
  - remove avoidable freedom
- The trader should shape the process around actual personality and constraints, not around fantasy identity.

## Main Lessons

- `Psychology is not edge`
  - If the method has no edge, mindset work will not save it.

- `Fit matters`
  - Market, time frame, and style should match temperament, schedule, and actual strengths.
  - A poor fit creates constant friction.

- `Know your real environment`
  - Different markets have different costs, noise, and execution demands.
  - The trader must understand the specific demands of the chosen arena.

- `Swing trading offers a useful middle ground`
  - It gives enough repetitions to learn, but usually with less pressure than day trading.
  - It also allows more work to be done outside market hours.

- `Consistency beats excitement`
  - Good trading is boring, repeatable, and disciplined.
  - Excitement usually comes from taking unnecessary risk.

## What This Changes For Me

- I should build my framework around who I actually am, not around the trader identity I imagine.
- If a style creates constant psychological strain, the answer may be to redesign the style rather than to force myself harder.
- Consistency in routine, journaling, and review is part of edge preservation.

## Rule Extracted

- `The best trading style is the one that fits my real personality, time constraints, and edge well enough to let me execute consistently.`
