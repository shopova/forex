## Core Idea

- The initial stop is one of the most important decisions in the trade.
- It defines:
  - the ultimate risk point
  - the size of the position
  - much of the emotional stability of the trade
- There is an unavoidable trade-off:
  - closer stop = larger size, lower win probability
  - wider stop = smaller size, higher chance of surviving normal noise

## Main Stop Types

- `Fixed-percentage stops`
  - Better than no stop.
  - Usually too crude because different markets have very different volatility.

- `Volatility-based stops`
  - More intelligent than fixed percentages.
  - Automatically adapt to the market's normal swing size.

- `Market-structure stops`
  - Best discretionary approach in Grimes' framework.
  - Stops should be placed beyond pivot points and beyond the level the market should not reach if the thesis is correct.

## Grimes' Practical Bias

- In general, initial stops should be fairly wide.
- Very tight stops often get hit by normal noise.
- If buying a sloppy support area or selling a sloppy resistance area, the stop often needs to go beyond the most extreme shadow / probe.
- If the fakeout extends further and then reverses, reentry may be necessary rather than pretending the first stop was wrong.

## What This Changes For Me

- A tight stop does not mean a lower-risk trade.
- A stop should sit beyond normal noise and beyond the point where the setup logic breaks.
- Structure first, volatility second, convenience never.

## Rule Extracted

- `My initial stop must be at a point the market should not reach if my trade thesis is correct.`

## Practical Forex Translation

- This supports your structural-invalidaton mindset directly.
- For your trades, the stop should usually sit beyond:
  - the pullback extreme
  - the key box edge / retest failure point
  - or the relevant liquidity sweep
- Then size the trade from that stop, not the other way around.
