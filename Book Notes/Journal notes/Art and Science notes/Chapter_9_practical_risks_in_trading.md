## Core Idea

- Some of the most dangerous risks in trading are not pattern-recognition problems.
- A trader can read the chart correctly and still lose through weak execution, hidden concentration, infrastructure failure, or trading an edge that no longer exists.
- Practical risk management means looking beyond the setup itself.

## Main Risk Categories

- `Trading without an edge`
  - This is the worst risk of all.
  - If there is no measurable edge, losses are not variance; they are the expected outcome.
  - Record keeping and honest performance analysis are mandatory.

- `Execution risk`
  - Intended price and actual fill price are often different.
  - Slippage, hesitation, missed trades, spread costs, and random size changes all erode the edge.

- `Disaster risk`
  - Power loss, Internet failure, hardware failure, broker access issues, or larger systemic disruptions can trap positions.
  - Backup plans matter.

- `Market evolution risk`
  - A strategy can stop working because conditions change.
  - Performance must be monitored so degradation is caught early.

- `Event risk / tail risk`
  - Rare shocks can produce moves far outside recent history.
  - These events are often unpredictable and expensive to hedge continuously.

- `Correlation risk`
  - Several separate positions can become one large bet if they move together.
  - Diversification often disappears when stress hits.

- `Liquidity risk`
  - Poor liquidity can turn a reasonable stop into a much larger realized loss.
  - Even normally liquid markets can become hostile during stress.

- `Regulatory risk`
  - Margin changes, short-sale restrictions, exchange decisions, or structural rule changes can damage execution and invalidate assumptions.

## What This Changes For Me

- I should review risk at three levels:
  - setup risk
  - account risk
  - operational risk
- It is not enough for a chart to look valid.
- I also need to ask:
  - can this be executed cleanly?
  - is my exposure concentrated?
  - am I trading a live edge or an outdated one?

## Rule Extracted

- `A valid chart setup is not enough; I must also control execution, concentration, and business-level risks around the trade.`

## Practical Forex Translation

- In FX, this means:
  - avoid stacking highly correlated USD exposures as if they were separate ideas
  - treat spreads, rollover, and news volatility as real execution costs
  - reduce trust in backtest-perfect fills around session opens and high-impact news
  - keep a clear rule for when poor recent execution or performance means the setup family needs review
- For your framework, the biggest practical risks are likely:
  - correlated pair exposure
  - spreads and slippage around news/session transitions
  - taking live trades before your sample proves the setup still has an edge
