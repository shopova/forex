# Section Note: Art and Science of Technical Analysis

Source:
- `Reading-materials/some-investment-books/New Folder/The Art and Science of Technical Analysis - Market Structure, Price Action, and Trading Strategies 2012 - annotated.pdf`

Parent chapter:
- `Chapter 9: Risk Management`

Section focus:
- `Misunderstood Risk`

## Core Idea

- Traders often misunderstand risk because they focus on one trade instead of a large sample.
- Real risk is a function of:
  - probability
  - consequence
  - asymmetry
- A normal loss inside a positive expectancy framework is not the same thing as true destructive risk.

## Main Concepts

- `Expected value matters more than trade-by-trade emotion`
  - Positive expectancy can come from:
    - a high win rate
    - a high reward/risk profile
    - or a workable combination of both
  - There is no built-in superiority in either style by itself.

- `Not every loss is true risk`
  - Over a large enough set of trades, routine losses are part of the cost of doing business.
  - The deeper risk is taking trades in a framework that does not actually have positive expectancy.

- `Humans handle low-probability, high-consequence risk badly`
  - People often ignore rare catastrophic events because they happen infrequently.
  - Traders confuse "unlikely" with "safe."

- `Sample size matters`
  - One trade proves almost nothing.
  - Tight stops may feel safer on a single trade, but across many trades they can create a constant stream of avoidable losses.

- `Uncertainty is not always bad risk`
  - Some risk measures punish all variability equally.
  - But upside-skewed variability is opportunity, not danger.
  - This is why simplistic volatility-based ideas can misread actual trading risk.

## What This Changes For Me

- I should judge my process over a meaningful sample, not by whether the next trade wins.
- I should stop calling a normal planned loss "bad risk" if it sits inside a valid expectancy framework.
- The real danger is hidden downside:
  - oversized loss
  - tail event
  - no edge
  - repeated small losses from bad structure choices

## Rule Extracted

- `A planned loss inside a positive expectancy process is a business expense; true risk is the kind of loss that can break the process or the account.`

## Practical Forex Translation

- In your trading, a clean stopped-out retest is not the main problem.
- The real problems are:
  - forcing entries with poor room
  - taking structurally weak setups with good-looking candles
  - using stops that are too tight for the pair's actual movement
  - increasing size emotionally because one setup "looks better"
- This chapter supports reviewing your edge over batches of trades in `R`, not over isolated wins and losses.
