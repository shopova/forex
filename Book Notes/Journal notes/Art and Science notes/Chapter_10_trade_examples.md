## Main Takeaway

- Chapter 10 shows how the book's patterns actually behave on real charts.
- The goal is not to memorize examples. The goal is to learn:
  - how context changes setup quality
  - how the right-side decision should be made with incomplete information
  - how failures and weak follow-through often teach more than perfect winners
- Clear risk definition, partial profits around `1R`, and disciplined exits matter more than finding dramatic home-run trades.

## Section Coverage

- `Trend Continuation`
  - Continuation trades are often the cleanest and most repeatable opportunities.
  - Higher-time-frame context can strengthen a simple pullback or breakout entry.
  - The swing trader's job is often just one clean leg, not endless hold time.

- `Trend Termination`
  - End-of-trend trades are more volatile and less clean than continuation trades.
  - Failure tests work best after a real change of character, not as blind fading.
  - Smaller size and strict partial-profit discipline are important here.

- `Failure Test Failures`
  - Countertrend failure tests can fail through drift, consolidation, second-entry sequences, or adverse gaps.
  - If the trade does not move quickly, risk should be reduced or the trade scratched.
  - Strong stop discipline is what keeps second-entry opportunities tradable.

- `Trading Parabolic Climaxes`
  - Parabolic zones are unstable and difficult to trade directly.
  - They are often better used as setups for failure tests, Anti patterns, or tight trailing exits on existing positions.

- `Trading at Support and Resistance`
  - Breakouts and failed breakouts are the main ways to trade levels.
  - Entry can come from the base, the actual breakout, or the first pullback after breakout.
  - The level itself is less informative than the price behavior around it.

## What This Chapter Changes

- I should stop studying only clean textbook winners.
- I need to spend more time on:
  - weak follow-through
  - failed patterns
  - second entries
  - realistic trade management after entry
- A valid setup is not just the trigger candle. It is the context, expected path, and behavior immediately after entry.

## Practical Forex Translation

- This chapter fits your work directly because it forces chart judgment, not rule memorization.
- For FX, the biggest translations are:
  - continuation trades should behave quickly and cleanly
  - countertrend entries need real structural justification
  - failed breakouts around session highs/lows and key levels are often more useful than breakout candles themselves
  - the first `1R` area is important for reducing risk, especially on reversals and volatile transitions

## Rule Extracted

- `The setup is only half the trade; the real edge comes from reading whether price behaves as expected immediately after entry and managing risk when it does not.`
