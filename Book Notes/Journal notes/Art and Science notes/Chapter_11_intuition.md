Parent chapter:
- `Chapter 11: The Trader's Mind`

Section focus:
- `Intuition`

## Core Idea

- Intuition is real, but it is not mystical and it is not a shortcut around disciplined study.
- It is trained pattern recognition operating below conscious thought.

## Main Lessons

- `Intuition grows from structured exposure`
  - Repeated contact with charts, records, and outcomes builds deeper pattern recognition.
  - It cannot be forced instantly.

- `Emotion can distort intuition`
  - Fear and greed communicate on the same internal channel that intuition often uses.
  - New traders usually cannot separate them cleanly.

- `Intuition is not an edge by itself`
  - Many people develop market intuition quickly.
  - What matters is whether that intuition is trained on valid structure and evidence.

- `Rational framework comes first`
  - Intuition should sit on top of rules, statistics, and observation.
  - It should not replace them.

## What This Changes For Me

- I should not romanticize gut feeling.
- If my intuition disagrees with my rules, I need to inspect whether it is insight or just emotion.
- Real intuition should become more trustworthy only after enough clean review and chart exposure.

## Rule Extracted

- `Intuition becomes useful only after disciplined exposure, emotional stability, and a rational framework that keeps it honest.`

## Practical Forex Translation

- This means:
  - do not override your setup rules because a chart "feels right"
  - let intuition act as a prompt for review, not as a standalone trigger
  - use journal review to see whether your intuitive overrides actually add value
- This fits your current process well because you already want cleaner, more objective setup families.
