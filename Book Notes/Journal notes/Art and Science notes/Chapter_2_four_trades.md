## 1. Core Idea

- What is the main concept from today?
  - Most price-action trades can be reduced to four structural categories: `trend continuation`, `trend termination`, `support/resistance holding`, and `support/resistance failing`.
  - The goal is to classify the trade correctly before choosing an entry trigger.
- What market problem is this concept solving?
  - It prevents mixing incompatible ideas, such as treating a continuation setup like a reversal, or shorting a level failure as if the level is still holding.

## 2. Market Condition Lens

Based on today's reading, how should the market be classified?

- Trend:
  - `Trend continuation` is the default trade in a healthy directional move.
  - `Trend termination` appears late in an aging trend and is effectively a reversal attempt.
- Range:
  - `Support or resistance holding` fits a market that is still respecting a boundary.
- Transition:
  - `Support or resistance failing` fits the moment when a level is losing control and the market may be moving from balance into imbalance.

Specific visual clues:

- Clean signs:
  - A clear sequence of higher highs and higher lows for bullish continuation, or lower highs and lower lows for bearish continuation.
  - A well-defined level that has been respected multiple times.
  - Strong rejection at a level for `holding`, or strong acceptance beyond a level for `failing`.
  - Room to the next meaningful opposing structure.
- Warning signs:
  - Overlapping candles near the level.
  - Breaks that occur but quickly snap back into the prior range.
  - A trend that is still moving, but with weaker follow-through and deeper pullbacks.
- Disqualifying signs:
  - No clear level to anchor the trade.
  - Entry trigger forms in the middle of a range.
  - The chart supports more than one trade type equally and the structure is unresolved.

## 3. Setup Interpretation

- Setup name:
  - `The Four Trades Framework`
- What must be true before the setup is valid:
  - The trade must be labeled as one of the four categories before entry logic is considered.
  - The chart must show a clear structural reason for that category.
  - The intended direction must match what price is doing at the key level.
- What makes the setup weak:
  - Calling a trade `continuation` when the trend is already unstable.
  - Treating a minor poke through a level as a real `failure`.
  - Taking `trend termination` trades too early without clear evidence that the prior trend has actually lost control.
- What would invalidate the idea:
  - A `holding` trade is invalidated if price accepts beyond the defended level.
  - A `failing` trade is invalidated if price breaks the level but cannot stay beyond it.
  - A `continuation` trade is invalidated if swing structure breaks against the trend.
  - A `termination` trade is invalidated if the prior trend resumes with fresh strength.

## 4. The Four Trade Types

- `Trend Continuation`
  - Trade with the existing trend after a pullback or pause.
  - Usually the highest-quality and most repeatable trade type for a developing trader.
  - Best when the trend is clean, pullbacks are controlled, and the next impulse has room.

- `Trend Termination`
  - Trade against the prior trend when evidence suggests the move is ending.
  - Higher difficulty because it depends on reading exhaustion, failed continuation, or reversal pressure.
  - Best handled selectively; weaker for rule-based execution unless structure is very clear.

- `Support / Resistance Holding`
  - Trade the level as intact, expecting rejection and a move back into the prior path.
  - Requires repeated respect of the level and a clear rejection signal.
  - Stronger when the level aligns with the broader market phase.

- `Support / Resistance Failing`
  - Trade the loss of control at a key level, expecting continuation through the break.
  - Requires more than a touch beyond the level; it needs acceptance, momentum, or a successful retest.
  - This trade is where false breakouts create the most damage if the read is wrong.

## 8. What I Misread Or Almost Misread

- A level being touched is not the same as a level `holding`; it needs actual rejection.
- A break through a level is not the same as a level `failing`; it needs acceptance beyond the level, not just a wick.
- `Trend termination` is attractive because it looks early, but it is usually less forgiving than continuation.

## 9. Rule Extracted Today

## Practical Forex Translation

- `Last Kiss` is usually a `support/resistance failing` trade followed by confirmation that the break is being accepted.
- `Big Shadow` can act as `support/resistance holding` or early `trend termination`, depending on where it forms.
- `Wammie/Moolah` is usually a `holding` or `termination` pattern, not a continuation pattern.
- For rule-based trading, favor `trend continuation` and clear `level failure` over early `trend termination`.
