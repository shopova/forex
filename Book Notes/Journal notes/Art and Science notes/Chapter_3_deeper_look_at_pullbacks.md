Parent chapter:
- `Chapter 3: On Trends`

Section focus:
- `A Deeper Look at Pullbacks`

## Core Idea

- Not every pullback is equal.
- Good pullbacks usually follow good momentum, stay corrective, and do not show major evidence of trend failure.
- Failed pullbacks often become the first warning that the market is shifting into range or reversal.

## Characteristics of Better Pullbacks

- Follows a strong prior impulse
- Does not follow a major momentum divergence
- Appears in a sensible place within the trend
- Retraces a reasonable portion of the prior move
- Looks corrective, not violent
- Shows lower-volatility behavior than the impulse

## Characteristics of Failed Pullbacks

- Prior impulse had weak momentum
- Pullback is too sharp countertrend
- New continuation leg fails near the previous pivot
- Pullback turns flat and loses conviction
- Sharp opposing momentum appears as the pullback is violated

## Trading Meaning

- Best use:
  - `Take continuation trades only when the pullback stays corrective and the prior impulse justifies continuation.`
- Avoid:
  - `Buying or selling continuation after a weak impulse, divergence, or already failed continuation attempt.`

## What This Changes For Me

- I should score pullback quality, not just trigger quality.
- A complex pullback is not just a deeper pullback; it is a structurally worse continuation environment.
- If the first attempt to resume trend fails, continuation quality usually drops.

## Rule Extracted

- `A pullback is only as good as the impulse before it and the behavior inside it.`

## Practical Forex Translation

- This is the chapter section most directly connected to your continuation filters.
- `Last Kiss` and other trend-following entries should be rejected if the pullback is already too sharp, too deep, or too structurally conflicted.
