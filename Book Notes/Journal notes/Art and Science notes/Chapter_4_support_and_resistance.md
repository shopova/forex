# Section Note: Art and Science of Technical Analysis

Source:
- `Reading-materials/some-investment-books/New Folder/The Art and Science of Technical Analysis - Market Structure, Price Action, and Trading Strategies 2012 - annotated.pdf`

Parent chapter:
- `Chapter 4: On Trading Ranges`

Section focus:
- `Support and Resistance`

## Core Idea

- Support and resistance are not magic lines.
- Their importance comes from how price reacts there, who may be trapped there, and what the larger structure suggests.
- The cleaner the line looks, the more careful I should be about stop clusters and false probes.

## Key Points

- Levels can be meaningful without being exact.
- Strong reactions, rejection, and acceptance matter more than line precision.
- Violations of a level do not automatically invalidate the range.
- `Spring` and `upthrust` are important examples of temporary violations that trap traders.

## What To Look For

- `Clean signs`
  - repeated reactions at the same area
  - strong rejection or clear acceptance
  - meaningful higher-time-frame alignment
- `Warning signs`
  - too many repeated tests
  - obvious stop clusters
  - weak reclaim after a probe
- `Disqualifying signs`
  - trading a level in the middle of a box
  - treating the line alone as the setup

## Trading Meaning

- Best use:
  - `Trade levels when behavior confirms holding, failing, or trapping.`
- Avoid:
  - `Treating support/resistance as automatic entries.`

## Rule Extracted

- `Support and resistance only matter when price behavior at the level proves they matter.`

## Practical Forex Translation

- A `Last Kiss` or range-edge trade should come from confirmed acceptance or rejection, not from touching a drawn line.
- If the level is obvious, assume stop-hunting risk is also obvious.
