# Chapter Note: Technical Analysis of the Financial Markets

Source:

Parent scope:
- `Priority chapters`

Chapter focus:
- `Chapter 17: Intermarket Analysis`

## Core Idea

- Markets do not move in isolation.
- Interest rates, commodities, bonds, equities, and currencies often influence each other through broader macro flows.

## Main Concepts

- `Cross-market context`
  - Strength or weakness in one market can provide useful background for another.

- `Macro relationships`
  - Bonds, rates, inflation-sensitive assets, and currencies often interact in recurring ways.

- `Context, not trigger`
  - Intermarket ideas are broad filters.
  - They are usually not precise enough to become standalone trade triggers.

- `Relationships can shift`
  - Correlations and lead-lag assumptions are not permanent.

## What This Changes For Me

- I should be aware of macro context without letting it override the chart.
- Intermarket thinking is useful for understanding the environment, not for forcing directional certainty.
- A chart setup still needs to stand on its own structure.

## Rule Extracted

- `Use intermarket relationships as background context, but let the actual trade decision come from the chart and the risk structure.`

## Practical Forex Translation

- This is relevant for FX because currencies sit close to macro flows.
- Useful applications:
  - knowing when bond or rate themes are dominating
  - understanding why USD strength or weakness may be broad
  - avoiding chart analysis that ignores obvious macro pressure
- But for your framework, this should remain background only. It must not displace setup quality, invalidation, or execution rules.
