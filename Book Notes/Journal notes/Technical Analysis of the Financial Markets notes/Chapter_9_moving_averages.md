## Core Idea

- Moving averages are organization tools.
- They help simplify trend direction, smooth noise, and frame dynamic support/resistance, but they are not a replacement for price structure.

## Main Concepts

- `Trend filtering`
  - Averages help show whether price is generally aligned up or down across a chosen period.

- `Dynamic support/resistance`
  - Price often reacts around watched averages, but the average matters only because traders are using it.

- `Crossovers and slope`
  - Crossovers and angle can offer broad information about trend state.
  - By themselves, they are often late.

- `Average selection`
  - Shorter averages respond faster but create more noise.
  - Longer averages are cleaner but slower.

## What This Changes For Me

- I should use averages as context filters, not as automatic entry signals.
- Their best use is to organize the chart and support structure reading.
- If the average says one thing and structure says another, structure should win.

## Rule Extracted

- `Use moving averages to simplify trend context, but never let them override actual price structure and invalidation.`

## Practical Forex Translation

- This supports your EMA-bounce and trend-filter logic, but with an important limit:
  - the EMA is helpful only when the broader structure already makes sense
- Good uses:
  - trend alignment
  - dynamic pullback reference
  - keeping chart clutter low
- Bad use:
  - taking trades just because price touched an average
