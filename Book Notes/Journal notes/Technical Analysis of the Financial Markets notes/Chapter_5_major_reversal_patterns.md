## Core Idea

- Reversal patterns matter because they show how existing trend pressure fails and a new auction begins.
- The point is not to memorize named formations blindly.
- The point is to understand when a trend is no longer acting like a healthy trend.

## Main Concepts

- `Top and bottom structures`
  - Head and shoulders, double tops/bottoms, and related patterns are classic ways trends lose structure.

- `Confirmation matters`
  - A pattern is not complete just because it looks familiar.
  - It usually needs a break of the defining support/resistance zone or neckline area.

- `Volume and conviction`
  - Classic analysis pays attention to participation around reversal structure.
  - Even if you do not rely heavily on volume in FX, the principle still matters:
    - failed continuation
    - weak follow-through
    - stronger move on breakdown or breakout

- `Measured implication`
  - Reversal patterns often imply a minimum projected move.
  - These projections are rough context tools, not guarantees.

## What This Changes For Me

- I should treat reversal patterns as structure shifts, not as pattern-label games.
- A reversal is more credible when the old trend has already weakened before the actual break.
- Without confirmation, many apparent reversals are just pauses.

## Rule Extracted

- `A reversal pattern becomes useful only when trend structure actually breaks and the market shows acceptance away from the old trend path.`

## Practical Forex Translation

- For your trading, this chapter is useful mostly as a language layer.
- It helps with:
  - identifying when a trend is distributing rather than continuing
  - seeing when repeated failure at highs/lows is meaningful
  - avoiding continuation trades late in deteriorating structure
- It should not tempt you into trading every visible double top or bottom without location, room, and confirmation.
