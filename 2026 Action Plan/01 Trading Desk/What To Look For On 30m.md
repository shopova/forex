# What To Look For On 30m

Use this note during live chart review.

Purpose:

- keep the `30m` in the right role
- stop vague entries
- remind you what must be visible before a trade becomes valid

Core rule:

- `Daily` gives the map
- `4H` gives the condition and zone
- `30m` gives the trigger

If the `30m` is trying to invent the trade by itself, there is no trade.

## 1. Meaningful Level

Ask:

- is price at a real `Daily` or `4H` level?
- does the level matter on the left side of the chart?
- is it a level you marked before the session?

Good:

- `Daily` or `4H` swing high / low
- repeated support or resistance
- prior breakout / failed-breakout area
- reclaim area

Bad:

- random `30m` line
- middle of chop
- level added after you already wanted the trade

## 2. Higher-Time-Frame Context

Ask:

- is the trade aligned with `Daily` / `4H`?
- if not aligned, is this at a real range edge or extreme?
- is this continuation, reclaim, breakout retest, or only local noise?

Good:

- aligned continuation
- clear reclaim from support or resistance
- real edge of a range with strong reversal evidence

Bad:

- mixed `Daily` and mixed `4H`
- mid-range reaction
- letting the `30m` do too much work

## 3. Acceptance

Ask:

- did price close through the level?
- did it stay on that side?
- did it start treating the level as support or resistance on the new side?

Bullish acceptance:

- close above
- hold above
- higher low above or near the level
- continuation can restart

Bearish acceptance:

- close below
- hold below
- lower high below or near the level
- continuation can restart

Not enough:

- one wick
- one dramatic candle with no follow-through
- repeated closes on both sides

Read more:

- [Edge Review and Setup Definitions.md](./../03%20Journal%20and%20Trade%20Reviews/Edge%20Review%20and%20Setup%20Definitions.md)

## 4. Reclaim

Ask:

- did price trade through the level first?
- did it then close back through it?
- did it then hold on the reclaimed side?

This is one of your best trigger families.

Read more:

- [Trigger Types and Definitions.md](./Trigger%20Types%20and%20Definitions.md)

## 5. Retest

Ask:

- after the break or reclaim, did price return in a controlled way?
- did the retest hold?
- did the retest stay shallow enough to preserve the idea?

Healthy retest:

- controlled pullback
- no violent rejection against the thesis
- continuation can restart

Bad retest:

- pullback too deep
- reclaimed area lost
- market turns back into chop

Read more:

- [Edge Review and Setup Definitions.md](./../03%20Journal%20and%20Trade%20Reviews/Edge%20Review%20and%20Setup%20Definitions.md)

## 6. Micro Structure Break

Ask:

- did price react at the level first?
- did it then break the last meaningful local pivot?
- did the break close through the pivot?

Good:

- break of last meaningful `30m` lower high for longs
- break of last meaningful `30m` higher low for shorts

Bad:

- tiny pivot break inside noise
- candle looks strong but no real pivot is broken
- break happens too late after the level reaction

Read more:

- [Trigger Types and Definitions.md](./Trigger%20Types%20and%20Definitions.md)

## 7. Impulse vs Correction

Ask:

- was the move into the level impulsive?
- is the current pullback weaker and more overlapping?
- or is the market just choppy everywhere?

Impulse:

- structure break
- expansion
- follow-through
- cleaner path

Correction:

- slower
- more overlap
- weaker candles
- retraces into a logical zone

Read more:

- [Impulse Identification Guide.md](./../../Reading%20Materials/Impulse%20Identification%20Guide.md)
- [Correction Quality Guide.md](./../../Reading%20Materials/Correction%20Quality%20Guide.md)

## 8. Post-Trigger Follow-Through

Ask:

- after the trigger, did price expand in the trade direction?
- or did it immediately overlap and stall?

Good:

- break, hold, continue
- clean movement within the next `1-3` candles

Bad:

- immediate overlap
- no progress
- instant reclaim against the idea

This is one of your biggest filters.

Read more:

- [Edge Review and Setup Definitions.md](./../03%20Journal%20and%20Trade%20Reviews/Edge%20Review%20and%20Setup%20Definitions.md)

## 9. Structural Invalidation

Ask:

- where is the trade actually wrong?
- is the stop above / below the structure that should hold?
- or is it only placed where pain starts?

Good:

- invalidation beyond defended swing
- invalidation beyond failed reclaim point
- invalidation beyond structure that defines the thesis

Bad:

- stop inside noise
- stop based only on small account discomfort

Read more:

- [Trigger Types and Definitions.md](./Trigger%20Types%20and%20Definitions.md)
- [Pre-Trade Entry Filter.md](./Pre-Trade%20Entry%20Filter.md)

## 10. Tradeable Space

Ask:

- is there still clean room to the next logical target?
- is there still at least `2R` after confirmation?

If confirmation destroys the `R:R`, skip.

## What Is Not Enough

These are not valid triggers by themselves:

- second touch
- hesitation
- one wick
- momentum looks tired
- one nice candle
- penetration without acceptance
- local `30m` move in mixed higher-time-frame structure

Read more:

- [Trigger Types and Definitions.md](./Trigger%20Types%20and%20Definitions.md)

## Fast Live Checklist

Before entry, ask:

1. meaningful level?
2. supportive `Daily` / `4H` context?
3. acceptance or reclaim?
4. controlled retest?
5. real micro structure break?
6. expansion after trigger likely?
7. invalidation structural?
8. clean `>= 2R` remains?

If any answer is `No`:

- no trade

## How Acceptance Turns Into An Entry

Acceptance is not the entry by itself.

Acceptance creates the condition for the entry.

Use these `3` main entry models.

### 1. Acceptance Candle Break Entry

Use when:

- price closes decisively through a meaningful level
- the acceptance candle is not too large
- the trade still offers clean `>= 2R`

Entry:

- buy stop above the bullish acceptance candle high
- sell stop below the bearish acceptance candle low

Stop:

- beyond the structure that should hold
- usually below the accepted level for longs
- usually above the accepted level for shorts

Good for:

- strong breakouts
- clean continuation after a reclaim
- fast session moves

Do not use when:

- the candle is too large
- entering above it ruins the `R:R`
- price is already extended into the next major level

### 2. Retest Hold Entry

Use when:

- price already closed through the level
- you want confirmation that the market can hold on the new side
- the first retest is controlled

Entry:

- after price pulls back and holds near the level
- enter on the first renewed push away from the level

Stop:

- beyond the retest low for longs
- beyond the retest high for shorts
- only if that stop still reflects real invalidation

Good for:

- calmer continuation trades
- breakout retests
- markets where immediate breakout entries are too aggressive

Do not use when:

- the retest goes too deep
- price slips back through the level and stays there
- the retest becomes chop

### When Consolidation Above A Level Is Healthy vs Weak

Sometimes price does not break and run immediately.

Sometimes it:

- breaks through the level
- holds above it
- pauses and builds
- then continues later

This can still be valid.

Healthy consolidation above a level:

- most candles stay above the level
- pullbacks remain shallow
- the level starts acting like support
- price is building, not collapsing
- the base forms on the new side of the level

Weak consolidation above a level:

- repeated closes on both sides of the level
- sharp drops back under the level
- wide messy chop around the level
- no sign that the level is being respected
- the market is drifting, not holding

Use this rule:

- if price is building above the level, the acceptance can still be valid
- if price is negotiating around the level, acceptance is weak or unclear

Entry logic:

- entry can still come on renewed continuation above the consolidation
- only if the stop remains structural and `>= 2R` still exists

Skip if:

- the consolidation becomes too large
- the base keeps slipping through the level
- waiting too long makes the trade too mature

### 3. Reclaim Plus Continuation Entry

Use when:

- price first trades through the level in the wrong direction
- then closes back through it
- then starts moving in the intended direction again

Entry:

- above the reclaim candle high for bullish reclaims
- below the reclaim candle low for bearish reclaims
- or on the first controlled retest after the reclaim

Stop:

- beyond the reclaim failure point
- beyond the defended swing that should not break if the reclaim is real

Good for:

- failed breakouts
- liquidity sweep reversals
- `Last Kiss` and failed-break responses

Do not use when:

- the reclaim close is weak
- price keeps closing on both sides of the level
- the reclaim is only one wick and no real close

## Which Entry Model To Prefer

Use this shortcut:

- strong clean breakout: `Acceptance Candle Break Entry`
- clean pullback after breakout: `Retest Hold Entry`
- failed break / sweep / false breakout: `Reclaim Plus Continuation Entry`

If you are unsure which one applies:

- the setup is probably not clean enough yet

## Final Rule

Do not enter because:

- the level was touched
- the candle looked strong
- the move felt obvious

Enter only when one of the entry models is actually present and the trade still keeps:

- meaningful level
- supportive context
- structural invalidation
- clean `>= 2R`
