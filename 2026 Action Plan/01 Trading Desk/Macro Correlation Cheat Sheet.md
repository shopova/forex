# Macro Correlation Cheat Sheet

Use this as a fast context guide during daily analysis. It is not a signal generator. Its job is to help classify whether a move is:

- pair-specific
- broad USD repricing
- risk-off
- de-escalation / relief
- inflation / commodity-driven

## Core Principle

Most major commodities are priced in `USD`.

Because of that:

- `USD down` often supports commodity prices
- `USD up` often pressures commodity prices

This is only the first layer. The real move may be driven by:

- geopolitics
- yields / rate expectations
- growth expectations
- inflation
- positioning / risk sentiment

## Main Instruments To Check

For live forex context, check:

- `DXY`
- `EURUSD`
- `GBPUSD`
- `USDJPY`
- `Gold`
- `Brent`
- `SPY` or `S&P futures`

Optional:

- `US 2Y / 10Y yields`
- `VIX`

## Dollar Relationships

### DXY

Use `DXY` as the first broad filter.

- `DXY down` = broad USD weakness more likely
- `DXY up` = broad USD strength more likely

If one USD pair is moving but `DXY` is flat, the move may be more pair-specific.

### EURUSD / GBPUSD / USDJPY

These help confirm whether the move is really broad USD repricing.

- `EURUSD up` + `GBPUSD up` + `USDJPY down` = broad USD weakness
- `EURUSD down` + `GBPUSD down` + `USDJPY up` = broad USD strength

If only one pair moves, be careful with macro conclusions.

## Gold

Gold reacts to:

- USD strength / weakness
- real yields
- uncertainty / hedging
- confidence in policy / central banks

Useful reads:

- `Gold up` + `DXY down` = USD weakness / hedging / lower real-yield pressure
- `Gold up` + `DXY up` = deeper fear / stress / strong safe-haven demand
- `Gold down` + `DXY up` = classic stronger-dollar pressure

Gold is context, not a trade trigger by itself.

## Brent / Oil

Oil is one of the most useful macro context tools, but it is very regime-sensitive.

Oil can rise because of:

- stronger demand / growth
- supply disruption
- war / geopolitical risk

Oil can fall because of:

- weaker demand
- de-escalation
- lower supply fear

Useful reads:

- `Oil up` + `Gold up` + `equities down` = geopolitical stress / risk-off more likely
- `Oil down` + `USD down` + `equities firm` = relief / de-escalation more likely
- `Oil down` after war headlines = market may be pricing less supply risk than expected

## SPY / Equities

Equities help separate panic from relief.

- `SPY up` or stable = market is not treating the event as deep risk-off
- `SPY down sharply` = stronger risk aversion / macro stress

Useful reads:

- `USD down` + `SPY up` = softer USD / relief / pro-risk tone
- `USD up` + `SPY down` = classic risk-off

## Commodity Currencies

### CAD

Often influenced by oil.

- `Oil up` can support `CAD`
- `Oil down` can weaken `CAD`

But broad USD moves can override that.

### AUD / NZD

Often influenced by:

- commodity demand
- China growth expectations
- risk sentiment

Strong global growth / commodity demand can support them.

## Practical Interpretation Templates

### 1. Broad USD Weakness

- `DXY down`
- `EURUSD up`
- `GBPUSD up`
- `USDJPY down`
- often `Gold up`

Meaning:

- macro-led USD selling
- not just a local technical breakout

Trading note:

- do not fade one pair’s move too aggressively if the whole USD complex confirms it

### 2. Classic Risk-Off

- `SPY down`
- `Gold up`
- often `USD up`
- sometimes `Oil up` if geopolitical

Meaning:

- fear is dominating
- safe havens are being bought

### 3. Relief / De-Escalation

- `SPY up`
- `USD down`
- `Oil down`
- `Gold can stay firm or cool slowly`

Meaning:

- market is removing immediate war / shock premium

### 4. Inflation / Supply Shock

- `Oil up sharply`
- inflation-sensitive assets react
- rate expectations can shift

Meaning:

- not every oil move is risk-off
- sometimes it is inflation repricing first

## How To Use This In Pair Analysis

When you see a strong candle in `EURUSD`, `GBPUSD`, or `USDJPY`, ask:

1. Is this move confirmed by `DXY`?
2. Are the other major USD pairs moving in the same direction?
3. Is `Gold` confirming uncertainty / hedging?
4. Is `Brent` confirming escalation or rejecting that story?
5. Are equities in panic, calm, or relief mode?

Then classify the move:

- local technical move
- broad USD repricing
- risk-off
- relief / de-escalation
- mixed / uncertain

## What Not To Do

- Do not treat correlations as fixed laws.
- Do not assume `gold up` always means `USD down`.
- Do not assume `oil up` always means `USD up`.
- Do not use one instrument alone to build a macro narrative.

Always look for a cluster of confirmation, not a single chart.

## Fast Trading Rules

- If `DXY` is falling sharply and at least two major USD pairs confirm USD weakness, be careful fading a local breakout.
- If `Oil` is falling and `SPY` is firm, the market may be pricing relief rather than escalation.
- If `Gold` is rising but `SPY` is stable, the move may be uncertainty / hedging rather than full panic.
- If a pair setup conflicts with the broad macro basket, require stronger technical confirmation.

## Final Reminder

Correlations help explain a move. They do not replace structure, levels, invalidation, or trade management.

Use macro context to answer:

- why is this moving?
- is the move broad or local?
- is it likely to continue or cool?

Then return to the chart and trade only if the setup still fits the plan.
