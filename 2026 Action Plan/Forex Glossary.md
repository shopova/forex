# Forex Glossary

This is a living glossary for practical forex terminology used in this workspace.

Core rule:

- prefer chart-usable definitions over vague textbook wording
- treat context words as decision aids, not automatic trade signals
- do not let a glossary term override structure, invalidation, or risk rules

## Momentum Divergence

Definition:

- price makes a new swing extreme, but the momentum measure does not confirm with a matching new extreme
- bullish divergence: price makes a lower low while momentum makes a higher low
- bearish divergence: price makes a higher high while momentum makes a lower high

What it usually suggests:

- directional pressure may be weakening
- the market may be approaching pause, pullback, or reversal risk

What it does not mean:

- not every divergence causes reversal
- divergence alone is not a valid trigger
- strong trends can keep running despite repeated divergence

In this framework:

- use it as supporting context only
- require actual behavior change such as a level reclaim, rejection at location, or a meaningful micro structure break before acting

## Climax / Climax Behaviour

Definition:

- a late-stage push where price accelerates aggressively in one direction and stretches away from normal rhythm
- it often reflects urgency, emotional participation, or forced exits from the losing side

Common signs:

- large expansion candles
- steeper slope than the prior trend leg
- little pause or pullback during the push
- price extends unusually far from moving averages or prior balance
- the move ends near obvious higher-time-frame structure or an extreme

Why it matters:

- climax behavior often appears near exhaustion points
- after a climax, the market is more vulnerable to sharp pullback, failed continuation, or transition into range

What it does not mean:

- climax behavior does not automatically mark the final top or bottom
- a strong trend can produce multiple climactic pushes before actually turning

In this framework:

- use it mainly as context for caution, profit-taking awareness, or reversal-quality assessment
- it is especially relevant when evaluating stretched moves behind `Big Shadow` style rejection setups

## Volatility

Definition:

- volatility is the degree and speed of price movement over time
- higher volatility means larger and faster swings
- lower volatility means smaller and slower swings

How it appears on chart:

- high volatility: wider candles, larger ranges, faster expansion, more stop-distance required
- low volatility: smaller candles, tighter ranges, slower movement, easier compression

Why it matters:

- volatility affects stop placement, position size, and whether a setup can still offer at least `2R`
- breakout quality changes with volatility: too little can mean no follow-through, too much can mean unstable retests or noise

In this framework:

- read volatility together with structure, not in isolation
- high volatility in clean context can support continuation
- high volatility inside chop usually lowers setup quality
- low volatility near key levels can precede expansion, but confirmation is still required

## Failure Test

Definition:

- a failure test happens when price probes beyond a meaningful level, swing, or edge of structure, but cannot stay accepted beyond it
- the market tests for continuation, fails, and then returns back through the level

What it usually suggests:

- breakout acceptance is weak or absent
- trapped participants may fuel a move back in the opposite direction
- the level is still being defended

What it looks like:

- price pushes through support or resistance
- follow-through is weak, brief, or immediately rejected
- price closes back inside the prior structure or back through the tested level

What it does not mean:

- one wick beyond a level is not enough by itself
- not every failed probe becomes a tradable reversal
- if price keeps accepting outside the level, it is not a valid failure test

In this framework:

- a failure test is context, then trigger only if behavior clearly changes
- the actionable part is usually the reclaim back through the level or the micro structure break that follows
- it is closely related to `Level Reclaim`, `failed breakout`, and some `Last Kiss` reversal cases

## Failure-Pullback

Definition:

- a failure-pullback is a pullback that happens after an initial failure test and reclaim
- after price rejects beyond a level and returns through it, the market pulls back toward that failed area before continuation away from it

What it usually suggests:

- the failed breakout or failed breakdown is being confirmed
- the market is retesting the rejection area from the opposite side
- trapped traders from the failed move may still support continuation

What it looks like:

- price breaks a level and fails to hold beyond it
- price reclaims back through the level
- then price pulls back toward the reclaim area in a controlled way
- continuation starts only if that pullback is rejected and acceptance remains on the reclaim side

What it does not mean:

- every pullback after a failed move is high quality
- if price drifts back into chop and loses the reclaim, the setup quality drops
- if the pullback becomes impulsive against the intended direction, it may no longer be continuation from failure

In this framework:

- treat it as a structured retest after a `Failure Test`
- the tradeable trigger is still the reclaim hold, rejection at location, or micro structure break
- it can overlap with `Last Kiss` logic when the failed breakout area becomes the retest zone
