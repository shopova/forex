## **Section 1: Core Structure** 

### **Overview: The 5–3 Rhythm**

The Elliott Wave Principle identifies that all price movements unfold in **five waves with the trend** (called *impulse waves*), followed by **three waves against the trend** (called *corrective waves*).  
 Together, they form one **complete cycle of eight waves**.

This rhythm appears consistently across all **timeframes and market degrees**, from monthly to minute charts, because market psychology is fractal — optimism and pessimism alternate in patterned sequences.

### **Wave Degrees**

Each wave pattern exists at multiple scales:

* **Grand Supercycle** → multi-decade trends  
* **Cycle** → 1–3 years  
* **Primary** → several months  
* **Intermediate / Minor / Minute** → days to hours

Each smaller degree mirrors the same 5–3 structure as the larger one — this is the **fractal nature** of markets.

## **Section 2: Impulse Waves — Rules & Fibonacci Ratios**

### **Overview**

An **impulse wave** is a five-wave pattern that moves in the direction of the dominant trend. Each impulse wave subdivides into **five smaller waves** (labeled 1–2–3–4–5), showing clear directional momentum and internal rhythm.

### **Rules (Must Always Hold)**

| Rule | Description |
| ----- | ----- |
| 1\. Wave 2 never retraces more than 100% of Wave 1 | Wave 2 can correct deeply but cannot move below the origin of Wave 1\. |
| 2\. Wave 3 is never the shortest | Typically the strongest and longest wave, showing momentum expansion. |
| 3\. Wave 4 never overlaps Wave 1 | The top of Wave 1 and bottom of Wave 4 must not overlap in price (except in diagonals). |

### **Guidelines (Usually True)**

| Guideline | Description |
| ----- | ----- |
| Wave 3 often extends 1.618× Wave 1 | Indicates powerful market participation. |
| Wave 5 ≈ Wave 1 or 0.618× Wave 1 | Common equality and ratio completion. |
| Wave 2 retraces 0.5–0.618× Wave 1 | Deep pullback due to early doubt. |
| Wave 4 retraces 0.236–0.382× Wave 3 | Shallow, slow correction due to sustained optimism. |
| Alternation Principle | If Wave 2 is sharp, Wave 4 is sideways (and vice versa). |

### **Fibonacci Ratio Table (Impulse Example)**

| Wave | Common Ratio | Measured From | Notes |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Wave 2 | 0.382 – 0.618 | Wave 1 | Corrective retracement |
| Wave 3 | 1.618 – 2.618 | Wave 1 | Main trend expansion |
| Wave 4 | 0.236 – 0.382 | Wave 3 | Alternating correction |
| Wave 5 | 0.618 – 1.000 | Wave 1 | Terminal push or exhaustion |

## **Section 3: Corrective Waves — Zigzags, Flats & Triangles**

### **Overview**

Corrective waves move **against the main trend** and typically unfold in **three-wave structures (A–B–C)**.  
 Unlike impulsive waves, corrections are **reactive** — they represent consolidation, indecision, and the temporary balance between buyers and sellers.  
 Corrections can vary in **form, depth, and complexity**, but they all serve the same function: to **retrace part of the preceding impulse** before the trend resumes.

### **Core Types of Corrective Patterns**

| Type | Structure | Description | Fibonacci Traits |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Zigzag (5–3–5) | Sharp, deep correction | Wave A and C are impulses; Wave B is short and corrective. | Wave B retraces \< 0.618 of A; Wave C ≈ 1.0–1.618 × A |
| Flat (3–3–5) | Sideways correction | Wave A and B are corrective; Wave C is impulsive. | Wave B retraces ≥ 0.9 of A; Wave C ≈ A or 1.618 × A |
| Triangle (3–3–3–3–3) | Contracting or expanding range | Represents market balance before breakout; occurs mostly in Wave 4 or B. | Each leg retraces 0.382–0.618 of prior leg |

### **Subtypes**

#### **1\. Zigzags**

* Sharp, single-directional countertrend correction.  
* Often appear in Wave 2 or Wave B of a larger correction.  
* **Double** and **triple zigzags** (W–X–Y, W–X–Y–X–Z) indicate prolonged corrections.

#### **2\. Flats**

* Broad, sideways formations.  
* Regular flat → B ends near A’s start.  
* Expanded flat → B exceeds A’s start; C extends sharply below A.  
* Running flat → B exceeds A’s start; C fails to move below A (rare, strong trend continuation signal).

#### **3\. Triangles**

* Five sub-waves labeled A–B–C–D–E, each subdividing into three waves.  
* Occur in Wave 4, Wave B, or Wave X positions.  
* Types: contracting, ascending, descending, or expanding.  
* Typically resolve with a **final thrust** in the direction of the larger trend.

## **Section 4: Ratio Analysis & Confluence**

### **Overview**

**Ratio analysis** lies at the heart of the Elliott Wave Principle.  
 It allows traders to identify **precise price targets** and **potential reversal zones** using Fibonacci relationships between waves.  
 When multiple Fibonacci ratios from different swings overlap, they create **confluence zones** — high-probability levels for entries or exits.

---

### **Key Ratio Concepts**

| Concept | Description | Common Use |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Retracement | Measurement of how far a correction moves against the prior trend. | Identify the end of corrective waves (2, 4, or B). |
| Extension / Projection | Measurement of how far an impulse extends beyond the previous wave. | Target continuation waves (3, 5, or C). |
| Equality | When two related waves are equal in length or time. | Used for symmetry — e.g., Wave C ≈ Wave A. |
| Confluence | When multiple Fibonacci levels align from separate measurements. | Marks strong support/resistance or reversal zones. |

### **Typical Fibonacci Relationships**

| Wave | Ratio | Measured From | Usage |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Wave 2 | 0.382 – 0.618 retracement | Wave 1 | Look for deep correction entries. |
| Wave 3 | 1.618 – 2.618 extension | Wave 1 | Main trend expansion zone. |
| Wave 4 | 0.236 – 0.382 retracement | Wave 3 | Sideways correction region. |
| Wave 5 | 0.618 – 1.000 extension | Wave 1 | Terminal target for impulse completion. |
| Wave C | 1.000 – 1.618 extension | Wave A | Completion of A–B–C correction. |

### **Confluence Example**

Imagine a bullish **EUR/USD** impulse:

* Wave 2 retraces 0.618 of Wave 1  
* Wave 4 retraces 0.382 of Wave 3  
* Wave 5 projects 0.618× Wave 1  
   If these levels cluster near the same price, they form a **Fibonacci confluence zone**, strengthening the likelihood of trend exhaustion or reversal.

### **Practical Ratio Combinations**

| Scenario | Combination | Implication |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| End of Wave C | Wave C \= 1.0× Wave A \+ 0.618 retracement of prior impulse | Strong completion zone for corrections |
| End of Wave 5 | Wave 5 \= Wave 1 or 0.618× Wave 1 | Potential terminal impulse |
| Wave 3 projection | Wave 3 \= 1.618× Wave 1 | Powerful trend extension |
| Multi-level confluence | Two or more Fibonacci levels from different waves align | Major pivot area — high-probability trade zone |

## **Section 5: Volume & Wave Personality**

### **Overview**

Volume and price structure reveal the *psychology* behind each wave.  
 Understanding this “personality” helps traders confirm whether a pattern is impulsive (trending) or corrective (consolidating).

| Wave | Personality | Volume Behavior |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Wave 1 | Initial move. Few believe it’s a new trend; skepticism dominates. | Modest, rising volume. |
| Wave 2 | Correction of Wave 1\. Bears regain confidence. | Decreasing volume. |
| Wave 3 | Broad recognition. The public joins the trend. | Strongest, highest volume. |
| Wave 4 | Consolidation. Traders take profits; optimism is still strong. | Volume contracts sharply. |
| Wave 5 | Final push. Often driven by emotion and late entries. | Volume lower than Wave 3, may show divergence. |
| Wave A | First countertrend move. Traders assume it’s a dip. | Volume moderate. |
| Wave B | Counter-correction, often deceptive. | Lighter volume. |
| Wave C | Completion of correction; emotional capitulation. | Volume rises again, then fades. |

## **Section 6: Guidelines — Scale, Volume, and the Right Look**

### **Overview**

Elliott emphasized that a correct wave count must have the **“Right Look.”**  
 It should *visually* fit the market’s rhythm — maintaining proportion, symmetry, and consistent scale.

| Principle | Description |
| ----- | ----- |
| Scale | Waves should look proportional; avoid counting tiny moves within large structures. |
| Volume | Confirms wave nature — expanding in impulses, contracting in corrections. |
| Right Look | The wave count should appear natural and fractal, not forced or irregular. |

### **Guidelines**

* Avoid labeling too many waves on small swings.  
* Maintain consistent degrees (don’t mix hourly and daily scales).  
* Channel impulses by connecting Waves 2 and 4; Wave 3 typically touches or pierces the upper boundary.  
* If a count “looks wrong,” it usually is — recheck proportions.

## **Section 7: Forex Application — Entry, Stop, and Target**

### **Overview**

The Elliott Wave Principle is most powerful when applied **strategically** in trending Forex markets.  
 It identifies *where you are* in the market cycle and *where to enter with low risk.*

### **Entry Strategies**

| Setup | Entry Zone | Stop Placement | Target |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| After Wave 2 retracement | 0.5–0.618 of Wave 1 | Below Wave 1 origin | 1.618× Wave 1 (Wave 3 projection) |
| After Wave 4 correction | 0.236–0.382 of Wave 3 | Below Wave 4 low | Equality with Wave 1 (Wave 5 target) |
| Post-C correction | End of Wave C confluence | Below C low | Begin new Wave 1 trend |

### **Risk Management**

* Keep stops beyond *invalid levels* (e.g., below Wave 1 origin in impulses).  
* Use **Fibonacci projection confluence** for profit targets.  
* Reduce position size during **corrective phases (A–B–C)**.

## **Section 9: Practical Trading Checklist**

**Step-by-Step Workflow**

1. Identify dominant trend and timeframe alignment.  
2. Begin preliminary wave count — mark 1–2–3–4–5 or A–B–C.  
3. Confirm wave proportions and alternation.  
4. Apply Fibonacci retracements to Waves 2 and 4\.  
5. Project extensions (1.618, 2.618) for Wave 3 and Wave C targets.  
6. Check volume for confirmation.  
7. Look for confluence between multiple Fibonacci levels.  
8. Enter near support/resistance confluence with clear invalidation.  
9. Manage position actively as subwaves develop.  
10. Trade what you see — not what you expect.

## **The “Last Kiss” Trading Setup (Price Action / Naked Trading)**

The **Last Kiss** refers to the **final retest** of a broken level (support or resistance) *before price continues strongly in the breakout direction*.  
 It’s sometimes called the **Breakout–Retest–Continuation pattern**.

---

###  **1\. The Core Concept**

Imagine price has been bouncing between support and resistance.

1. Price **breaks through** a key level (support or resistance).  
2. It then **retests** that same level from the opposite side — this retest is the *“last kiss”*.  
3. The market then **continues** in the breakout direction.

So, it’s literally price “kissing goodbye” the level before moving away.

---

### **2\. Chart Conditions**

* **Clean chart** — no indicators.  
* **Key horizontal level** clearly defined (support/resistance).  
* Can be used on **4H, 1H, or Daily** timeframes for best results.

## **3\. Step-by-Step Setup: “Last Kiss” (Breakout–Retest–Continuation)**

### **1\. Identify the Consolidation Zone**

* Draw a **box** around an obvious **consolidation area** on the chart.  
* The zone should have **at least two touches** on both support and resistance levels.  
* This box defines your **support and resistance boundaries**.

---

### **2\. Signal for the Trade**

* Wait for **price to move beyond** (break out of) the consolidation zone.  
* Then, wait for a **retest** (price pulling back) to the **edge of the zone** that was broken.  
* Look for a **strong candle** forming in the **direction of the breakout**:  
  * Bullish candle → signal for **buy setup**  
    * Bearish candle → signal for **sell setup**

---

### **3\. Entry Rules**

* **Buy Setup:**  
* Place a **Buy Stop** order **above the high** of the next **bullish** candlestick.  
* **Sell Setup:**  
* Place a **Sell Stop** order **below the low** of the next **bearish** candlestick.

---

### **4\. Emergency Stop Loss**

* Calculate the **width of the consolidation box**:  
   `Width = Resistance Level − Support Level`  
* Divide that width by **2**.  
* Add this amount to the **support level zone** to determine your **emergency stop loss**.

---

### **5\. Profit Target**

* Set your **take profit** at the **nearest major support or resistance zone** in the direction of your trade.  
* Optional: Trail stop to lock in profits if price moves strongly in your favor.

# **Big Shadow — Quick Trading Guide**

### **Pattern Definition**

Two-candle reversal setup:

* The **second candle (Big Shadow)** has a **higher high & lower low** than the previous candle.  
* It’s **the largest candle** in the last **5–10 bars**.  
* Appears at **zones** or **extreme highs/lows**.

---

### **Valid Setup Checklist** 

* ☐ Forms **on a support/resistance zone**  
* ☐ **Biggest candle** in recent history (5–10 bars)  
* ☐ **Close near the high** (bullish) or **near the low** (bearish)  
* ☐ **Room to the left** (no price action for \~20–30 bars)  
* ☐ Appears **after a clear move**, not in chop

---

### **Bullish Big Shadow**

* **Entry:** Buy Stop above high of Big Shadow  
* **Stop-Loss:** Few pips below its low  
* **Target:** Next resistance or 2–3× risk

### **Bearish Big Shadow**

* **Entry:** Sell Stop below low of Big Shadow  
* **Stop-Loss:** Few pips above its high  
* **Target:** Next support or 2–3× risk

---

### **Management Tips**

* Move **SL to breakeven** after \+1R  
* Cancel order if not triggered within **3–5 candles**  
* Avoid trading during **high-impact news**

---

### **A+ Big Shadow Traits**

*  At key zone  
* Dominant candle  
* Clean close near edge  
* Clear “space” to left  
* Triggers within a few bars

# **Wammie & Moolah Trading Strategy (Naked Forex Method)**

---

# **Overview**

**Wammies** and **Moolahs** are reversal patterns that occur at **strong support and resistance zones**.  
 They rely on **two touches of a zone**, a specific **price behavior between touches**, and a **catalyst candlestick** on the second touch.

These patterns incorporate core market truths about reversals:

* Markets often reverse after **two consecutive touches** of a zone  
* **Higher lows** → bullish continuation  
* **Lower highs** → bearish continuation  
* Bullish candle on support → strength  
* Bearish candle on resistance → weakness  
* “Room to the left” enhances reversal probability  
* Well-defined zones are essential  
* More candles between touches \= stronger setup

---

# **Wammie (Bullish Reversal Pattern)**

## **Wammie Characteristics**

1. **Support zone touched twice**  
2. The **second touch is higher** than the first (higher low)  
3. **Minimum of 6 candlesticks** between the two touches  
   * More is better (20+ is ideal)  
4. On the second touch, the market prints a **bullish catalyst candlestick**  
   * Examples: bullish candle, Big Shadow, Kangaroo Tail  
5. **Entry:** Buy Stop placed a few pips **above** the bullish catalyst candle  
6. **Stop-loss:** a few pips **below** the first (lower) touch  
7. **Profit Target:** next **resistance zone** above the Wammie

---

## **Wammie: Additional Rules & Filters**

### **Market Truth Integration**

* Two touches on a zone → often leads to a reversal upward  
* Higher second touch → market showing signs of strength  
* Bullish candle on support → strong reversal signal

### **Quality Filters**

* Prefer Wammies with:

  * **20+ candles** between the two touches  
  * **Strong catalyst** (Big Shadow, Kangaroo Tail)  
  * **Second touch significantly higher**  
  * **Strong and obvious support zone**  
  * **No nearby zones** to maximize reward  
  * **Room to the left** (little price action before the pattern)

---

# **Moolah (Bearish Reversal Pattern)**

## **Moolah Characteristics**

1. **Resistance zone touched twice**  
2. The **second touch is lower** than the first (lower high)  
3. **Minimum of 6 candlesticks** between touches  
   * More candles strengthens the setup  
4. On the second touch, the market prints a **bearish catalyst candlestick**  
5. **Entry:** Sell Stop placed a few pips **below** the bearish catalyst candle  
6. **Stop-loss:** a few pips **above** the first (higher) touch  
7. **Profit Target:** next **support zone** below the Moolah

---

## **Moolah: Additional Rules & Filters**

### **Market Truth Integration**

* Two touches on a zone → reversal likelihood increases  
* Lower high → market losing bullish momentum  
* Bearish candle on resistance → strong downside signal

### **Quality Filters**

Choose Moolahs that feature:

* **20+ candles** between touches  
* A powerful catalyst:  
  * Bearish Big Shadow  
  * Bearish Kangaroo Tail  
* A second touch **much lower** than the first (clear weakness)  
* A **strong, important resistance zone**  
* Large “**room to the left**”  
* No nearby support that would limit profit potential

---

**Trade Management**

## **Entry Style**

* Always use **stop entries**, not market orders  
  * Buy Stop for Wammie  
  * Sell Stop for Moolah

## **Stop Loss Placement**

* Always beyond the **first touch**, not the second  
  * Wammie → SL below first touch  
  * Moolah → SL above first touch

## **Take Profit**

* The nearest **opposing zone** is the target  
  * Wammie → next resistance  
  * Moolah → next support
