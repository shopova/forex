It is a test of:

- loss control
- consistency
- discipline under pressure
- ability to stop trading when needed

Your records show the opposite pattern during the damaged period:

- overtrading
- oversized losses

- become the kind of trader who could pass FTMO without forcing it

## 2. Current Official FTMO Reference Points

These are the current public FTMO standards that matter most.

### FTMO Challenge: 2-Step

Core public objectives:

- Profit Target: `10%` in the Challenge
- Maximum Daily Loss: `5%`
- Maximum Loss: `10%`
- Minimum Trading Days: `4`
- Trading Period: unlimited

Verification then uses:

- Profit Target: `5%`
- while the loss limits remain the same

### FTMO Challenge: 1-Step

Core public objectives:

- Profit Target: `10%`
- Maximum Daily Loss: `3%`
- Maximum Loss: `10%`
- Best Day Rule: one day cannot exceed `50%` of total profit
- Trading Period: unlimited

The `1-Step` model is more direct, but it is not easier for an undisciplined trader.

For your current profile, the `1-Step` model is more dangerous because:

- the daily loss limit is tighter
- the Best Day Rule punishes unstable burst performance
- your current weakness is behavioral volatility

## 5. Your Personal Readiness Gates

These are the gates you must pass before paying for FTMO.

They are intentionally stricter than FTMO’s own limits.

### Gate 1: Risk Discipline

You must complete at least `30` consecutive live or micro-live trades with:

- no single loss bigger than planned `1R`
- no stop widening
- no unplanned re-entry
- no day where you break your own daily stop

If this is not true, you are not ready.

### Gate 2: Frequency Control

For at least `6` consecutive weeks:

- maximum `2` trades per day
- maximum `6` trades per week
- maximum `1` open position at a time
- no impulsive same-pair re-entry

If your behavior still expands under stress, you are not ready.

### Gate 3: Environmental Control

For at least `30` consecutive trading days:

- no phone entries
- all trading from desktop
- one planned session only
- no trading when emotionally unfit

If you cannot keep the environment controlled, you are not ready.

### Gate 4: Structural Trading Only

For at least `30` consecutive trades:

every entry must be based on:

1. `Daily` bias
2. clean `4H` impulse
3. healthy correction
4. valid `1H` trigger

If an entry is taken because of a signal alone, it does not count.

### Gate 5: Expectancy

Over a sample of at least `30-50` trades:

- net result must be positive
- average loss must stay controlled
- no outlier loss may wipe out more than `3` average winners

A high win rate alone does not qualify you.

### Gate 6: Emotional Stability

For at least `30` days:

- no revenge trading
- no trading to recover a red day
- no rule-breaking after a loss
- no emotional urge to "make the account back"

If urgency still drives entries, you are not ready.

## 6. The FTMO Entry Sequence You Should Use

Do not go straight from rebuild to paid challenge.

Use this sequence:

1. Complete the `90 Day Live Rebuild Plan`
2. Review the final `30` days only
3. If all readiness gates are met, take an FTMO Free Trial
4. Treat the Free Trial like a real evaluation
5. Only if the Free Trial is traded cleanly, consider paying for a challenge

Passing the Free Trial does not prove full readiness.

Failing to trade it cleanly proves you need more preparation.

## 7. Which FTMO Product Fits You Better Later

If you do become ready, the safer path for your profile is:

- start with the classic `2-Step` challenge, not the `1-Step`

### Why the 2-Step Is Better for Your Current Behavioral Profile

- the rules are simpler
- the Maximum Daily Loss is `5%`, not `3%`
- there is no Best Day Rule complication
- it better rewards consistency over gambling for a fast result

### Why the 1-Step Is Not the Right First Choice for You

- tighter daily loss leaves less room for execution mistakes
- the Best Day Rule can pressure you psychologically
- your past issue was unstable behavior, not lack of access to a faster model

If you cannot pass the `2-Step` mentally, the `1-Step` will not solve the real problem.

## 8. How You Must Behave on an FTMO Account Later

Before you ever pay for a challenge, your mindset must already be:

- I do not need to trade every day
- I do not need to recover today
- I can stop after one bad trade
- I can follow the plan when nothing is happening
- I can accept missing a move

If any of these feel false right now, do not buy the challenge yet.

## 9. Red Flags That Mean "Do Not Buy Yet"

If any of these are present, delay FTMO:

- you still want the challenge mainly to prove something
- you still feel urgency to recover old losses
- you still check charts compulsively from the phone
- you still feel better after many trades than after one clean trade
- you still size emotionally after a red day
- you still trust indicators more than structure

One of these is enough to pause.

## 10. Green Flags That Mean "You Are Getting Close"

These are the signs of actual readiness:

- you can go several days with no trade and feel fine
- your losses look planned, not chaotic
- your journal is clean and specific
- your position size stays stable
- you no longer need many trades to feel productive
- you can explain every trade in one structured sentence
- your process remains stable after a loss

That is what readiness looks like.

## 11. Personal FTMO Readiness Scorecard

Score each item `Yes` or `No`.

- `30+` consecutive trades with no oversized loss
- `6` consecutive weeks within frequency rules
- no phone entries for `30+` trading days
- positive expectancy over the most recent `30-50` trades
- no revenge day in the last `30` days
- no rule violation in the last `30` days
- can stop after one invalid trade
- can accept no-trade days
- all recent trades taken from structure, not signals alone
- daily emotional state is calm before trading

If fewer than `8/10` are `Yes`:

- do not buy FTMO yet

If `8/10` or more are `Yes`, and the missing items are minor:

- test yourself on a Free Trial first

If `10/10` are `Yes` and the Free Trial is traded cleanly:

- you may consider a paid `2-Step` challenge

## 12. Bottom Line

You should not buy FTMO because you want relief, proof, or a reset.

You should buy FTMO only when:

- your process is already stable
- your losses are already controlled
- your behavior is already consistent

FTMO should test an existing professional process.

It should not be the tool you use to create one.
